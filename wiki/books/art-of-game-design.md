## The book

Jesse Schell's "The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses" — the field's most-used design framework text. Not a how-to but a *way of seeing*: 100+ "lenses" (named design questions) you apply to a game to inspect it from different angles.

## The core frameworks

### The elemental tetrad
Every game is four things: **Mechanics** (rules/systems), **Story** (narrative), **Aesthetics** (look/feel), **Technology** (what enables it). A change to one reverberates through the others — a useful lens on every design decision (our "mechanics-first" posture is itself a tetrad choice: tech + mechanics lead, aesthetics/story emerge).

### The lenses (most relevant to game2)

| Lens | Question it asks | Our application |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential Experience** | what feeling is this game about? | the feeling of *a living village* — the sim is the feeling |
| **Surprise** | what will surprise and delight? | `emergent-storytelling.md`, `small-events-big-impact.md` |
| **Natural Interface** | does interaction feel physical/intuitive? | indirect control — influence, not command |
| **Meaningful Choice** | are choices real? | `trolley-problem-mechanics.md` dilemmas |
| **Challenge** | right difficulty? | the balance rubric (`fun-rubric-automated-testing.md`) |
| **Fairness** | do players feel it's fair? | consequences traceable to choices/state |
| **Skill vs Chance** | right mix? | skill in systems-understanding; chance in events |
| **Imagination** | does it spark the player's mind? | emergent stories + named villagers do this work |
| **Interest Curve** | pacing of engagement | event cadence, crisis/recovery rhythm |
| **Endogenous Value** | do things matter *in-game*? | land/relationships/reputation as real value |
| **Community** | does the game build one? | in-game community mechanics literally |
| **Simplicity + Emergence** | complex results from simple rules? | the core design bet |

### The design process chapters

- **Idea → prototype → test → iterate** — the loop our agent fleet runs (`component-testing-framework.md`, `1000-turn-testing.md`)
- **The problem is the design** — Schell's "the game is the solution to a problem" framing — our problem: *can a simulated village feel alive?*

### The "player's mind" chapters

- Experience > mechanics — the player doesn't play systems, they play *the experience the systems produce* — the design argument for emergence over authored content

## Application to game2
