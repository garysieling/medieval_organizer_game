# Delegated Agency Through Characters

**Category:** Design / Player Interface
**Source:** Backlog item (High) — "Delegated agency through characters: autonomously-acting subordinates."
**Date:** 2026-10-25

## Description

How the player governs a growing realm when they can't influence everyone directly: they appoint *people* — stewards, bailiffs, reeves, captains, priests, guildmasters — who then exercise their own agency under the delegated authority. Delegation is the middle layer between god-game control and society sim.

## The delegation model

- **Office ≠ person:** a position (steward) has authority scope + duties; a person (villager with traits) holds it. The office defines *what they may do*; the person decides *how*.
- **Delegation is trust:** the player picks a person for an office, and thereafter governs *through* their competence and character — a lazy steward yields a lazy village, a loyal-and-capable one runs itself
- **Authority decays with distance:** delegated agents act on local info and their own judgment; the player's intent attenuates the farther the agent is (courtiers hear intent; a border reeve interprets)
- **Accountability, not control:** the player can't micro-manage a steward, but can *inspect* (reports, visits, audits), *correct* (advice, reprimand), *replace* (dismiss — with social/political cost)

## Delegation dynamics

| Dynamic | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| **Competence** | delegated agents use their psych/skill stats — a great bailiff is worth real production |
| **Loyalty & interest** | agents pursue their own interests within scope (embezzlement, favoritism, family nepotism) — the principal-agent problem as a game mechanic |
| **Drift** | long-held offices accumulate power/faction — a steward becomes a patron, builds a clientele, might resist removal |
| **Reporting** | what reaches the player is what agents *report* — fog of war applies to governance; auditors and visitors get truer info |
| **Delegation chains** | large realms are nested delegations (lord→bailiff→reeve) — each layer a leak of intent and a source of drift |

## Why it's the scale mechanic

`village-to-kingdom-scale.md` is powered by delegation: a village is manageable directly; a town needs a steward; a kingdom is a delegation tree. The *depth* of the tree is the price of scale — and the source of court politics, corruption, and coups.
