## Purpose

The user asked for a list of Freire-adjacent authors, researched and synthesized. The cluster turns out to be one coherent project: **education as the mechanism by which domination is reproduced or undone** — which makes it the direct theoretical complement to the Foucault material (disciplinary institutions produce subjects; counter-institutions produce counter-subjects) and the practical complement to IAF organizing (house meetings ARE Freirean praxis).

## The List (generated, organized by what each adds)

### The core
1. **Paulo Freire** — *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (1970). Banking vs. problem-posing education; conscientization; praxis (reflection+action); the oppressor-within; fear of freedom; cultural invasion vs. cultural synthesis.
2. **Augusto Boal** — *Theatre of the Oppressed* (1979). Freire's method embodied: spectators → "spect-actors"; Forum Theatre (rehearse solutions to staged oppression), Image Theatre, Legislative Theatre; the "Joker" facilitator.
3. **Myles Horton / Highlander Folk School** — *We Make the Road by Walking* (with Freire). The American practitioner who did it before Freire theorized it — founded 1932; trained Parks, King, Baker; the Citizenship Schools (Septima Clark, Esau Jenkins, Bernice Robinson): literacy→vote, teachers must be community members, "the exam was registering to vote" (80% of Robinson's students registered). Aldon Morris's term: the **"movement halfway house"** — an org without mass base that generates skilled activists, tactical knowledge, and movement memory.

### The parallel critiques
4. **Ivan Illich** — *Deschooling Society* (1971). The libertarian dark twin: institutional schooling itself domesticates — it confuses learning with schooling, produces dependency, certifies obedience. Alternative: "learning webs," skill exchanges, convivial tools. Pairs exactly with Foucault's carceral continuum.
5. **Pierre Bourdieu & Jean-Claude Passeron** — *Reproduction* (1970). Pedagogy as "symbolic violence": schooling reproduces class relations *by appearing neutral* — cultural capital converts inherited advantage into "merit." The academic critique of the same institution Illich attacked politically.
6. **Antonio Gramsci** — *Prison Notebooks*. Hegemony (rule by consent manufactured in civil society); **organic intellectuals** (every class produces its intellectuals — the movement's job is to produce them from the ranks, not import experts); **war of position vs. war of maneuver** — the slow counter-institution trench war vs. the frontal assault. The strategic spine for all of this.

### The applied wings
7. **bell hooks** — *Teaching to Transgress* (1994), *Feminist Theory: From Margin to Center*. Engaged pedagogy; the margin as counter-hegemonic site; education as "the practice of freedom"; the classroom as the most radical space of possibility.
8. **Gustavo Gutiérrez / liberation theology** — *A Theology of Liberation* (1971). Preferential option for the poor; base ecclesial communities (CEBs) — Freirean praxis in church form: lay communities "reading their own reality" through scripture (literally Exodus — the text the Slave Bible deleted). Counter-move documented: Vatican silenced Boff (1985), Latin American security states assassinated clergy (Romero 1980 — shot mid-Mass).
9. **Orlando Fals-Borda** — Participatory Action Research (PAR). Research *as* organizing: La Rosca trained communities as their own investigators — "critical recuperation": recover the buried history of popular struggles and redeploy it. (Notably: our literature-review methodology is PAR's move.)
10. **Henry Giroux / Peter McLaren** — US second wave. Critical pedagogy as academic field; public pedagogy; education against neoliberalism.

### The counter-point (intellectual honesty)
11. **Jacques Rancière** — *The Ignorant Schoolmaster* (1987, on Jacotot). The challenge to Freire: emancipation isn't *given* by a liberating teacher — the teacher who "explains" stultifies by presuming inequality of intelligence. Jacotot's discovery: students learned what he couldn't teach — emancipation = acting on the **axiom of equal intelligence**, not on a method. The useful tension: is conscientization still teacher-led?

### Deep precursors/siblings
12. **Du Bois** — double consciousness (the interior split of the dominated — pairs with Fanon's colonial psychology and the broker-dilemma in the faith doc); the Talented Tenth debates (elite education as advancement vs. separation — the same debate as the mission-school comprador backfire).
13. **John Dewey** — progressive education (the liberal precursor; learning by doing — Highlander and folk schools descend from this + Danish folk-school model).

## The Eight Insights That Matter (synthesis across the cluster)

### 1. Education is never neutral — it domesticates or liberates
Freire's core: "banking" education (depositing content into passive students) *is* the practice of domination — it treats people as objects to be filled, which rehearses obedience. The **form** teaches before the content does: a lecture about liberation is a contradiction; a dialogue about oppression is a rehearsal of agency. The container shapes the content — which is why the Slave Bible's *form* (redacted, read-only) mattered as much as its cuts.

### 2. The oppressor-within is the deepest layer of domination
Freire + Fanon + Du Bois converge: the oppressed internalize the oppressor's image of themselves — fear of freedom, self-deprecation, the desire to *become* the oppressor rather than become free. Liberation is therefore first a **psychological mechanic**, then a political one. Organizing fails when it asks people to act as subjects they don't yet feel themselves to be. (This is the `internalized domination` stat — see mechanics.)

### 3. Praxis is a loop, not a sequence
Reflection → action → reflection. Action without reflection = activism (burnout, drift); reflection without action = verbalism (the theory club, the seminar). Freire's critique of both failure modes. The BECs formalized it: see-judge-act. It's also exactly the structure of a good campaign debrief — the praxis loop is what turns experience into capacity (the "losses teach if org survives" finding from round 1, now with a theory).

### 4. Teachers must come from the community
Highlander's rule: no certified teachers in Citizenship Schools — Bernice Robinson (a beautician/seamstress without a diploma) out-taught every professional. Gramsci's organic intellectual: the movement's educators must be *of* the constituency — same language, same stakes, same skin in the game. Imported experts can't produce emancipated subjects — they reproduce hierarchy in liberating dress. (The IAF's "organic leaders," the mission-school backfire — same rule from three directions.)

### 5. The product is agency, not information
The exam was *registering to vote*. Success isn't knowledge transferred but transformed actors — people who now act as subjects. This is the metric correction for all education/economy systems: measure the disposition change (courage, initiative, critical analysis), not the content transfer.

### 6. Institutions produce subjects — so movements build counter-institutions
The Foucault–Freire synthesis: if prisons/schools/asylums manufacture docile subjects, counter-institutions manufacture resistant ones. The historical inventory: folk schools (Highlander), citizenship schools, freedom schools (1964), base communities, workers' education, movement presses, theatre of the oppressed. **Building the institution IS the organizing** — the school is simultaneously service, recruitment, leadership pipeline, and doctrine-transmission in one structure.

### 7. Research/education can BE organizing
PAR (Fals-Borda): the community investigates its own conditions — the research process itself recruits, develops leadership, and produces actionable knowledge. Citizenship Schools were indistinguishable from movement recruitment infrastructure. The boundary between "education project" and "organizing" is artificial — which is exactly why power attacks both (the Slave Bible; firing Septima Clark; banning Tuscon's Mexican-American studies program — documented modern case).

### 8. Embodied rehearsal matters (Boal's contribution)
Forum theatre = rehearsal for revolution — you practice interventions against staged oppression *in the body* before doing them for real. The spect-actor is literally a training mechanic: low-cost practice of the high-stakes move. Charivari/carnival inversion (sprint-7) is the folk version — ritualized reversal as practice-space for inversion that might become real.

## Where the Cluster Cuts Against Itself (honest tensions)

- **Freire vs. Rancière**: is conscientization still a teacher-student hierarchy? Rancière says the liberating teacher reproduces the inequality it claims to undo — the only truly emancipatory move is *assuming* the student's intelligence, not bestowing consciousness. Practical resolution used in the field: Highlander's "we teach each other" + community teachers — answer the objection structurally, not rhetorically.
- **Freire vs. Illich**: Freire wants to *transform* institutions; Illich wants to *dissolve* them. Both agree the institution's current form is the problem; they split on whether the form can be redeemed.
- **Horton vs. Freire** (in their dialogue): Horton was suspicious of theorizing his practice — "the answers come from the people," theory follows practice. The tension between method and spontaneity in the cluster itself.

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: `internalized_domination` as a citizen stat
Deep stat: reduced initiative, resistance to joining (fear of freedom), and — the Fanon detail — susceptibility to *becoming* petty oppressors (the driver who over-enforces). Lowered only by consciousness-raising actions (house meetings, literacy, witnessing wins). It's the hidden stat explaining why raw grievance ≠ mobilization.

### Mechanic 2: The praxis loop for skill growth
Characters/org grow capacity via alternating reflection↔action: `action` events produce `experience`; `reflection` activities (debrief meetings, study circles) convert experience to `skill`. Action-only grinding plateaus; reflection-only produces theorists not organizers. This models "power builds as you spend it" (sprint 7) + "losses teach if org survives" (round 1) in one loop.

### Mechanic 3: Community-teacher requirement
Education infrastructure (schools, circles, trainings) produces `critical_agency` — but ONLY if staffed by community members (organic-intellectual rule): imported experts produce `dependency` instead. Player tradeoff: community teachers are slower to find/train; imported experts are fast but build the wrong stat.

### Mechanic 4: Spect-actor rehearsal actions
Theatre/ritual/roleplay actions: low-stakes practice runs that build `courage` + `discipline` at fraction of real-action cost — forum theatre, drill nights, march rehearsals, mock pickets. Rehearsed orgs pass discipline checks at higher rates (the Otpor training effect, generalized).

### Mechanic 5: Movement schools as org buildings
`folk_school`/`study_circle` building type: outputs leader development, doctrine coherence, member retention, and `movement memory` (preserves tactics across generations/defeats — the halfway-house function: the school IS how the org survives demobilization). Connects to the enforcement doc: institutions that outlast campaigns.

### Mechanic 6: Oppressor-within flip risk
Deep-dominated citizens who gain power without conscientization have elevated `become_oppressor` risk — the driver's over-enforcement, the collaborator class, the revolutionary-turned-tyrant (Münster's van Leiden). Education is the immunization mechanic.
