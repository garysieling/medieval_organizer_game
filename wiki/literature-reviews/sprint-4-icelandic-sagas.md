# Lit Review - Sprint 4: Icelandic Sagas

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Medieval Literature / Legal Anthropology
**Sources:** Miller (Bloodtaking/Peacemaking), Byock (Feud in Saga), Cold Cases (legal detail), Conflict of Law & Justice

## Sources

### 1. Miller - Bloodtaking and Peacemaking: Feud, Law, Society
- **Feud + law + peacemaking bound together** - one couldn't get even without going one better
- **Stateless society:** ordering/disordering impulses, negotiated violent possibility
- **Social control via law** (not central authority) in a sophisticated republic
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - feuds as mechanics, law as social control, vengeance cycles

### 2. Byock - Feud in the Icelandic Saga
- **Feud channeled into accepted patterns:** conflict -> advocacy -> resolution (feudemes)
- **Land = source of conflict** (property disputes)
- **Narrative structure = feud structure:** saga = conflict organized
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - feuds as narrative + mechanic, land as conflict source

### 3. Cold Cases: Law and Legal Detail
- **Legal technicalities drive narrative** - mistakes, court cases, loopholes as plot
- **"Saga law" as building blocks** - coherent rules emerge from cases
- **Relevance:** HIGH - legal detail as story mechanic, rules emerge from cases

### 4. Conflict of Law and Justice
- **Longest republic (870-1262):** free men + formidable women testing legal codes
- **Competing principles:** pagan v Christian, vengeance v compromise, monarchy v republicanism, courts v arbitration
- **Tragic choices:** no simple solutions, profound legal philosophy
- **Relevance:** HIGH - competing legal/principle choices as gameplay

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **Feud cycle:** offense -> vengeance -> escalation -> peacemaking -> resolution (or continued)
2. **Can't get even without going one better** - vengeance escalates, never equalizes
3. **Law as social control** - sophisticated legal code replaces central authority
4. **Legal technicalities** - mistakes, loopholes, precedents drive conflict
5. **Peacemaking:** arbitration, advocacy, brokered resolution
6. **Land/property = conflict source** - disputes over territory/ownership
7. **Competing principles:** vengeance vs compromise, old vs new law, different authorities
8. **Free people + strong women** - agency across gender, testing legal codes

## Design Proposals

- **Feud system:** offense -> vengeance -> escalation -> peacemaking/resolution (cycles)
- **Vengeance escalation:** getting even = going one better (never equalizes)
- **Law as control:** sophisticated legal code, not central authority
- **Legal cases as stories:** technicalities, loopholes, precedents drive narrative
- **Peacemaking:** arbitration, advocacy, brokered resolution
- **Land disputes:** territory/property as recurring conflict
- **Competing principles:** players choose between legal/moral frameworks
- **Strong characters across roles:** women, priests, outlaws, warriors all agents

## Rating
**Critical** - Sagas directly model our vision: feuds as emergent cycles, law as social control, legal detail as narrative driver, peacemaking, land as conflict source, competing principles. Perfect for emergent storytelling.
