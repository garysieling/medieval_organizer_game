# Lit Review: Pre-Modern Resistance Organizations — Underground Churches, Peasant Wars, and the Inquisition Playbook

**Survey Date:** 2026-10-31
**Agent:** Literature Survey Agent
**Domain:** Medieval Religious/Social Movements, Heresy & Persecution History
**Series:** Organizing research round 3 (user-requested)
**Confidence:** High — grounded in inquisitorial records (among the best primary sources in medieval history)

## Purpose

Which org forms survived centuries of suppression, and what the medieval counter-intelligence playbook looked like. The medieval period is a goldmine for this because **the repression institution kept meticulous records** — the Inquisition's dossiers are our best primary sources on how underground communities actually worked.

## Part 1: The Survivors and the Almost-Winners

### Waldensians (1170s–present) — the durability champion
- Valdes of Lyon (c. 1173): wealthy merchant gives away wealth, preaches apostolic poverty — same founding as Francis, opposite fate: refused preaching license, driven underground.
- **The clandestine order**: itinerant "barbes" (preachers) traveling in disguise, holding secret schools, hearing confessions, maintaining a lay network — a parallel church with no buildings. Peter Biller's framing: "between a religious order and a church."
- **Survival tech**: no fixed target (no churches to burn), lay-embedded (members stay Catholic publicly — *dissimulation* as doctrine, confessing to parish priests while secretly Waldensian), Alpine refuges as deep infrastructure, transmission through family + itinerant preaching.
- **Survived**: 800+ years — through crusades, inquisitions, massacres (Mérindol 1545, Piedmont Easter 1655 — which provoked Milton's famous sonnet and Cromwell's diplomatic intervention — early international solidarity!), absorbed into Protestantism, still exist today.
- **Lesson:** the most durable underground form isn't a cell network — it's a *parallel church*: full-service institution (schools, clergy, confession, mutual aid) hidden inside the host population.

### Hussites (1415–1436) — the rare negotiated win
- Jan Hus burned at Constance 1415 (safe-conduct violated — the classic negotiate-and-behead: come talk, we'll kill you) → Bohemia explodes.
- **The military innovation**: Jan Žižka's wagon-forts — armored war wagons chained into mobile fortresses; peasant infantry with flails defeated four crusades. Tactical innovation beating feudal cavalry (cf. Flint sit-down: tactics that neutralize the power's weapon).
- **The split that decided everything**: moderate Utraquists vs radical Taborites. Catholics negotiated with the moderates → **Compactata of Basel (1436)**: communion in both kinds granted, church property concessions — then the moderates + Catholics together crushed Tabor at Lipany 1434.
- **THE recurring pattern**: when the movement has moderate and militant wings, the enemy's optimal move is *settle with the moderates* — splitting the movement and making the moderates the repressors. (Same move as "controlled opposition by co-option" in the countertactics doc — the moderate wing isn't fake opposition, but it's usable.)
- **Lesson:** a movement's internal split is the lever the enemy pulls — unity is the scarcest resource at negotiation time.

### Lollards (1380s–1520s)
- Wycliffite network: vernacular Bible production + lay preachers ("poor priests") — an **information infrastructure movement** first (scripture in English was the weapon).
- After Oldcastle's failed 1414 rising, went fully underground for a century — persisting through family transmission and artisan networks, feeding into English Reformation. Documented in episcopal registers as durable local cells.
- **Lesson:** text-based movements persist — a book is a leader that can't be arrested.

### Remença (Catalonia, 1462–1486) — the rare peasant WIN
- Catalan serfs (payeses de remença) ran two wars + decades of negotiation against the "evil customs" (mals usos) — abusive feudal dues and lordly jurisdiction.
- **Won**: the Sentencia Arbitral de Guadalupe (1486) — the crown's arbitration abolished serfdom and the evil customs in exchange for retaining some dues. One of the very few medieval peasant movements to *win its stated demand* through a negotiated settlement.
- **Why it won:** sustained organization across two wars + the crown as a *usable* arbiter (royal power needed the peasants against the nobility — a pillar-defection: the movement pulled the crown away from the nobles).

### Franciscans vs Dominicans — the institutional fork
- **Francis (1209)**: prophetic poverty movement that the Church *absorbed* — made it an order, then split it (Spiritual Franciscans persecuted by their own institution for insisting on actual poverty). The **capture-by-incorporation** case: the Church's genius move was granting the movement a licensed form, then policing the license. This is the medieval version of the controlled-opposition seat — a real seat, with rules.
- **Dominicans (1216)**: founded to *counter* the heresies — trained preachers to out-argue Cathar and Waldensian doctrine. The counter-movement order literally staffed the Inquisition. **Counter-organizing insight:** medieval power's answer to movement organizing was *parallel organizing* — monks who matched the heretics' mobility, poverty credibility, and preaching skill.

## Part 2: Communal & Insurrectionary Forms (the failures that still teach)

- **Bundschuh conspiracies (1493–1517)**: repeated peasant plots under the peasant's-shoe banner — a symbol-as-flag network across the Upper Rhine, suppressed each time, feeding cumulative grievance into 1525. Repeated failed conspiracies = a movement that keeps its network warm between attempts.
- **English Peasants' Revolt (1381)**: John Ball's sermon networks ("When Adam delved and Eve span...") — preaching tours as the organizing channel for 20 years before the rising. The revolt collapsed the moment **Wat Tyler was killed at the negotiation table** — the negotiate-and-decapitate countertactic, older than Huss: the king grants an audience, the audience is the trap.
- **Jacquerie (1358)**: spontaneous explosion after noble collapse at Poitiers — no org, no program, annihilated in weeks by coordinated noble response. What *pure* spontaneity buys you.
- **Ciompi (Florence, 1378)**: wool-workers' uprising — briefly seized the city, established a radical guild, dissolved within months through elite-outwait + internal collapse. Near-miss: an actual urban worker government, but with no supply of political skill (they didn't know how to *be* the government — the movement-to-administration gap).
- **Landsgemeinde (Swiss peasant assemblies)**: open-air voting assemblies — the durable *institutional* form of peasant self-governance; survived because embedded in canton structures (institutional anchoring again).

## Part 3: The Inquisition — medieval counter-intelligence, documented

**Why this matters for the game:** the Inquisition is the best-documented counter-organizing institution of the pre-modern world — and its methods are eerily modern.

### The record system (Montaillou, 1318–1325)
- Bishop **Jacques Fournier** (later Pope Benedict XII) ran a rigorous diocesan inquisition; his **register of depositions survives** — the source for Le Roy Ladurie's *Montaillou*, the deepest microhistory of a medieval village ever written *because the interrogation files are that detailed*.
- The register documents the counter-network: informers planted in villages, penitents flipped into denouncers, houses watched, travelers questioned. The Pamiers Waldensian cell (1319) was dismantled through this network — four of Fournier's five career executions came from that one cell.

### The playbook (documented in inquisitorial manuals — Bernard Gui's *Practica Inquisitionis*, 1320s, is literally a counter-organizing handbook)
1. **General sermon + grace period**: summon the town, preach, offer ~1 month of grace to confess and inform — harvest the network's membership cheaply, with confessions naming names. *The amnesty is the dragnet.*
2. **Secret denunciation**: accusations taken in secret, accusers' names withheld — the accused never confronts the witness (the snitch-jacket's mirror: secret accusation is safe accusation).
3. **Relapse = death**: heretics who recant get penance — *if they relapse, they die*. Creates a permanent informer class: the relapsed have everything to lose and everything to gain by informing. Engineered loyalty through fear — a flipped-agent program.
4. **Dossier keeping**: testimony recorded verbatim, cross-referenced, held for years — medieval case files enabling longitudinal network mapping.
5. **Self-appointed inquisitors tolerated**: freelance heresy-hunters (documented 1390–1404 German anti-Waldensian campaigns) — outsourcing repression to motivated civilians (see: Mohawk Valley citizens' committees).
6. **Penitent-turned-agent** deployment: converted heretics sent back to identify networks (the bleuite precedent, 600 years early).

### What the Inquisition reveals about counter-organizing
- **Information asymmetry is the whole game**: the Inquisition's power was its *records* — it knew the network better than the network did. (Modern parallel: the informant is the phone.)
- **The Church competed on the movement's own terms**: Dominicans out-preached, confessed, and traveled like the heretics — counter-organizing is organizing.
- **Tolerance of the imperfect instrument**: inquisitors were often corrupt, self-appointed, or brutal — the institution absorbed them anyway because the function mattered more than the method.

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Underground church form
A `hidden_institution` type: provides full services (teaching, ritual, mutual aid, confession) while dissimulated inside the host population. High durability, low offensive capacity — the Waldensian tradeoff: survive forever, never govern.

### Mechanic 2: The Inquisition counter-cycle
Power-holder AI runs the documented sequence: summon → grace period (amnesty harvest) → informer recruitment → prosecution wave → relapse-informer maintenance. Counter-counter: `security_culture` reduces harvest yield; Waldensian-style dissimulation defeats surface compliance checks but not planted informers.

### Mechanic 3: Relapse mechanic
Flipped members are a tracked resource for the enemy — penitents under threat who must inform or die. Orgs counter via `verification norms` + cutting known-exposed members loose *with support* (reduces the enemy's leverage — compassion as counter-intelligence).

### Mechanic 4: Negotiate-and-decapitate
Dangerous event type: power offers negotiation; the parley is the ambush (Tyler 1381, Hus 1415). Player counter: negotiate at distance, hostages/guarantees, or distributed delegation. The safe-conduct violation is a *legitimacy-costly* move for power — documented that Constance's betrayal scandalized Europe.

### Mechanic 5: Moderate-militant split exploitation
At negotiation time, power can offer partial settlements that satisfy moderates — splitting the movement, and potentially turning moderates into repressors of militants (Lipany). Unity before negotiation is the counter — and it costs.
