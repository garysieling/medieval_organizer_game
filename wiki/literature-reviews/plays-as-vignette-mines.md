# Plays as Vignette Mines Literature Review

**Survey Date:** 2026-11-14
**Issue:** #1211
**Agent:** Literature Survey Agent
**Confidence:** High
**Domain:** Drama — Shakespeare, medieval mystery plays, classical comedy

## Summary

Plays are pre-tested vignettes: small casts, sharp stakes, a turn, a payoff — built to land on a crowd in one scene. The medieval mystery plays are doubly relevant because they were *staged by villagers like ours* (guild pageants on carts); their comedy is village-scale by construction. The five strongest stagable finds: a theft concealed by a domestic bluff, friendship tested at the scaffold, a plea against the law's letter, a marriage rowed in the street, and the stranger who isn't what they say. Each maps to an existing staging system (rare event, dilemma, meeting beat, talk arc).

## Key Sources & Scenes

### The Second Shepherds' Play (Wakefield/Towneley cycle, ~15th c.)
**Rating:** A — primary text, the best-preserved village farce
**Scene → #1254:** Mak steals a sheep, swaddles it in a cradle as a newborn; the shepherds search his cottage while his wife Gill plays the recovering mother. The bluff breaks on a *kindness*: they stop to leave the "bairn" a gift and see the sheep.
**Why it works:** crime + cover-story + search + reveal sprung by generosity — a complete arc that flatters the investigator's heart, not their suspicion.
**Staging:** rare event + dilemma; the reveal mechanic (a kindness cracks the play) is the transferable bit.

### Everyman (~1510)
**Rating:** A — primary text, the reckoning-of-friends scene
**Scene → #1255:** Summoned to death, Everyman asks Fellowship, Kindred, and Goods to walk with him; all refuse; only Good Deeds — weak from neglect — goes. Friendship isn't stated in the scene, it's *tested* by it.
**Why it works:** the ledger made visible — you learn exactly who every bond was worth by watching who steps forward.
**Staging:** the condemned's last walk (hanging/stocks/exile); read the attendance straight off the relationship ledger; who stands feeds reactions.rs sympathy spread.

### Merchant of Venice / Measure for Measure (Shakespeare)
**Rating:** A — primary texts, the mercy-plea trial form
**Scene → #1256:** A rigid sentence, a public pleading, and the law's letter turned against itself (Portia's pound-of-flesh technicality; Isabella's trade-baited appeal). Two variants in one form: the *plea* and the *sharp answer*.
**Why it works:** sentencing is a drama of bystanders — the crowd's temper, the pleader's standing, and the wrong's weight are all visible variables.
**Staging:** a dilemma at sentencing under community_law; player's word moves the crowd; the technicality is a rare smart-out.

### The Noah plays (Wakefield, York, Chester cycles)
**Rating:** A− — primary texts; the domestic-argument scenes are the comic peaks
**Scene → #1257:** Noah's wife won't board the ark — a household disagreement staged in the street with neighbors watching. In Wakefield she boxes his ears; in York her gossips bring her drink.
**Why it works:** a private conflict made public, and the watchers *take sides* — instant texture that does real opinion work.
**Staging:** a rare Moment event seeded from real household disagreement; witnesses tilt by their own bonds; can be about the cause itself.

### Plautine/Shakespearean disguise engine (Plautus → Comedy of Errors → Henry IV)
**Rating:** B+ — a mechanism more than a scene
**Scene → #1258:** The stranger who isn't what they say. Our Visitors (tinker, minstrel, troupe) are all honest; the false one belongs to counter-intel.
**Why it works:** the tell must be *learnable not flagged* — the player who talks to strangers catches it; the honest mechanic is that conversation is the detection instrument.
**Staging:** a Visitor variant whose cry doesn't match its questions; spotting it opens say-nothing / expose / feed-a-story.

### Also surveyed, not proposed
- **Jack Cade / Coriolanus crowd scenes:** a demagogue riding a petitioning crowd — our meeting/campaign beats already stage the room-turning shape; a dedicated mob event would duplicate the campaign arc.
- **Lysistrata (Aristophanes):** the coordinated household strike — the cause's strike/refusal machinery covers the mechanics; the comedy is in execution, not a missing system.
- **Tavern temptations (Mankind, Henry IV):** an alehouse plot pulls a member — covered in spirit by the counter-pressure/informer layer and the false-traveller proposal.
