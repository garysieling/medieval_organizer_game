## Summary

Medieval medicine centered on the Four Humors theory (Hippocrates, Galen) where health depended on balancing blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile. Monasteries preserved herbal knowledge through physic gardens cultivating 50+ medicinal plants. Apothecaries prepared remedies using mortars, distillation, and humoral theory. Common herbs like willow bark (proto-aspirin), feverfew (headaches), and yarrow (wounds) had genuine biological activity. Dangerous plants (hemlock, nightshade, mandrake) served as both poisons and medicines. This research provides rich mechanics for herb cultivation, remedy crafting, humoral diagnosis, and poison/antidote systems.

## Key Sources

### Four Humors Theory (Hippocrates & Galen)
**Rating:** A+
**Type:** Historical Medical Theory / Classical Medicine
**Relevance:** High - Core diagnostic framework

**Key Findings:**

**The Four Humors:**
1. **Blood** (hot & wet) - associated with air, spring, sanguine temperament
2. **Phlegm** (cold & wet) - associated with water, winter, phlegmatic temperament
3. **Yellow Bile** (hot & dry) - associated with fire, summer, choleric temperament
4. **Black Bile** (cold & dry) - associated with earth, autumn, melancholic temperament

**Theoretical Framework:**
- **Health = Balance:** All four humors in proper proportion
- **Disease = Imbalance:** Excess or deficiency of one or more humors
- **Diagnosis:** Observe symptoms to determine which humor is out of balance
- **Treatment:** Restore balance using herbs, diet, bloodletting, purging

**Origins:**
- **Hippocrates (460-370 BC):** Initially developed theory
- **Galen (129-216 AD):** Expanded into comprehensive medical system
- **Medieval Period:** Dominated European medicine for 1000+ years
- **Influence:** Lasted into early modern period (1600s)

**Galen's Theory of Opposites:**
- If too much phlegm (cold & wet) → eat hot peppers (hot & dry)
- If too much blood (hot & wet) → bloodletting or cold/dry foods
- Match remedy properties to opposite of disease properties

**Treatments Based on Humors:**

**Depletory Regimen (Reduce Excess):**
- **Bloodletting:** Remove excess blood (most common for fever, inflammation)
- **Cupping:** Create suction to draw out bad humors
- **Blistering:** Raise blisters to expel toxins
- **Purging:** Laxatives to expel bile via intestines
- **Vomiting:** Emetics to expel phlegm via stomach
- **Sweating:** Heat treatments to expel via skin

**Herbal Remedies (Balance Humors):**
- Herbs classified by hot/cold, wet/dry properties
- Choose herbs with opposite properties to imbalance
- Example: Cold disease → warm herbs (ginger, pepper)

**Source Quality:** Well-documented historical medical system, primary sources available
**Game Application:** Diagnostic system with humoral balance mechanics

### Apothecaries & Preparation Methods
**Rating:** A
**Type:** Historical Profession Study / Pharmaceutical History
**Relevance:** High - Remedy crafting mechanics

**Key Findings:**

**Role of Apothecaries:**
- **Profession:** Herbalists, healers, pharmacists of medieval period
- **Locations:** Bustling marketplaces, monastery infirmaries
- **Skills:** Botanical knowledge + medical theory
- **Products:** Salves, tinctures, poultices, powders, decoctions

**Preparation Methods:**

**1. Grinding (Mortars & Pestles):**
- Crush herbs, roots, minerals into powders
- Essential for mixing ingredients
- Different textures for different uses

**2. Infusions:**
- Steep herbs in hot water
- For leaves, flowers (delicate parts)
- Like making tea
- Short duration (5-20 minutes)

**3. Decoctions:**
- Boil herbs in water
- For roots, bark, tough materials
- Longer duration (20-60 minutes)
- Extracts deeper constituents

**4. Poultices:**
- Crushed herbs applied directly to skin
- Often warm, wrapped in cloth
- For wounds, inflammation, pain
- Examples: Yarrow for wounds, comfrey for broken bones

**5. Distillation:**
- Extract essential oils from plants
- Concentrated active ingredients
- Required specialized equipment
- For perfumes, potent remedies

**6. Tinctures:**
- Herbs soaked in alcohol or vinegar
- Long shelf life (preservation)
- Concentrated medicine
- Drop-by-drop dosing

**7. Salves & Ointments:**
- Herbs mixed with fats (lard, beeswax, butter)
- Topical application
- Heal skin, wounds, rashes
- Example: Calendula salve for wounds

**Tools of the Trade:**
- Mortar and pestle (grinding)
- Distillation apparatus (extraction)
- Scales (precise measurement)
- Jars and vials (storage)
- Knives (harvesting, cutting)
- Drying racks (preservation)

**Theoretical Basis:**
- Chose herbs based on humoral properties
- "Cold" disease → "warm" herbs
- Balanced ingredients to target specific humor
- Combined theory with practical experience

**Source Quality:** Historical descriptions, archaeological finds, recipe books
**Game Application:** Crafting system with multiple preparation types

### Medieval Medicinal Herbs & Their Uses
**Rating:** A
**Type:** Ethnobotany / Historical Pharmacology
**Relevance:** High - Specific herb database

**Key Findings:**

**Primary Source: Dioscorides' *De Materia Medica* (65 AD):**
- 600+ plants documented
- Medicinal applications described
- Basis for medieval herbalism until 1500
- Widely circulated in medieval West and Arab world

**Common Ailment Categories:**

**1. Digestive Remedies:**
- **Chamomile** (*Matricaria chamomilla*): Stomach pain, gas, indigestion
- **Mint** (*Mentha*): Upset stomach, nausea, digestive aid
- **Fennel** (*Foeniculum vulgare*): Gas, bloating, appetite control

**2. Respiratory Treatments:**
- **Thyme** (*Thymus vulgaris*): Coughs, colds, bronchitis
- **Hyssop** (*Hyssopus officinalis*): Respiratory infections, expectorant
- **Horehound** (*Marrubium vulgare*): Persistent coughs, asthma

**3. Pain & Fever:**
- **Willow Bark** (*Salix*): Fever, aches, pain (contains salicin, proto-aspirin)
- **Feverfew** (*Tanacetum parthenium*): Fevers, headaches, migraines ("medieval aspirin")
- **Poppy** (*Papaver somniferum*): Severe pain, sleep (opium source)

**4. Wound Healing:**
- **Yarrow** (*Achillea millefolium*): Battle wounds, bleeding, infection (knight's herb)
- **Calendula/Marigold** (*Calendula officinalis*): Skin wounds, inflammation
- **Comfrey** (*Symphytum officinale*): Broken bones, sprains (knitbone)

**5. Skin Conditions:**
- **Chamomile** (*Matricaria chamomilla*): Irritation, antiseptic
- **Lavender** (*Lavandula*): Burns, minor wounds
- **Calendula** (*Calendula officinalis*): Rashes, eczema

**6. Mental/Neurological:**
- **Rosemary** (*Rosmarinus officinalis*): Memory, concentration
- **Lavender** (*Lavandula*): Anxiety, insomnia, headaches
- **St. John's Wort** (*Hypericum perforatum*): Melancholy (depression)

**7. Dangerous/Controlled:**
- **Mandrake** (*Mandragora*): Anesthetic, sleep (highly toxic)
- **Henbane** (*Hyoscyamus niger*): Pain, sleep (poisonous)
- **Opium Poppy** (*Papaver somniferum*): Severe pain (addictive)
- **Deadly Nightshade** (*Atropa belladonna*): Sleep draught (fatal in excess)

**Preservation Methods:**
- **Drying:** Herbs hung or laid flat, stored up to 1 year
- **Powdering:** Dried herbs ground, mixed into preparations
- **Fats:** Added to lard/wax for ointments (preserves longer)
- **Alcohol:** Tinctures have indefinite shelf life

**Modern Validation:**
- Medieval pharmacopeia shows biological activity against infections
- Ingredients repeatedly combined for specific symptoms
- Evidence of experimental basis, not just folklore
- Some remedies genuinely effective (willow bark, yarrow, chamomile)

**Source Quality:** Historical herbals, modern pharmacological studies, ethnobotany
**Game Application:** Herb database with specific ailment mappings

### Medieval Poisons & Antidotes
**Rating:** A
**Type:** Toxicology History / Forensic Medicine
**Relevance:** Medium-High - Poison mechanics for intrigue/warfare

**Key Findings:**

**Common Poisons:**

**1. Hemlock** (*Conium maculatum*):
- **Family:** Carrot family (deceptively similar to parsley)
- **Effects:** Extreme dizziness, peripheral numbness spreading inward, paralysis of heart and lungs, death
- **Timeline:** Hours to kill
- **Historical Use:** Execution method (Socrates drank hemlock in prison)
- **Active Compound:** Coniine (neurotoxin)

**2. Deadly Nightshade/Belladonna** (*Atropa belladonna*):
- **Medieval Name:** "Dwale" (Chaucer)
- **Uses:** Sleeping draught (small doses), poison (large doses)
- **Effects:** Dizziness, dry mouth, flushing, nausea, visual impairment, increased heart rate, agitation, raving, followed by weakness, sleepiness, respiratory failure, death
- **Dual Nature:** Medicine or poison depending on dose

**3. Mandrake** (*Mandragora officinarum*):
- **Category:** Strong sedative/anesthetic
- **Effects:** Deep sleep, delirium, respiratory depression, death (high doses)
- **Folklore:** Root shaped like human, screamed when pulled from ground
- **Medical Use:** Pre-surgery anesthetic

**4. Henbane** (*Hyoscyamus niger*):
- **Group:** Alongside opium, mandrake, hemlock as dangerous sleep medicines
- **Effects:** Drowsiness, hallucinations, delirium, death
- **Risk:** Narrow margin between therapeutic and lethal dose

**5. Aconitum** (*Aconitum napellus* - Monkshood/Wolfsbane):
- **Toxicity:** Extremely poisonous
- **Effects:** Numbness, vomiting, heart arrhythmia, death
- **Historical Use:** Poison arrows, assassination

**6. Yew** (*Taxus baccata*):
- **Toxic Parts:** All except red aril (fleshy seed covering)
- **Effects:** Cardiac arrest, respiratory failure
- **Symbolism:** Associated with death, graveyards

**Historical Antidotes:**

**Mithridatium:**
- Complex universal antidote containing dozens of ingredients
- Named after King Mithridates VI who took small poison doses to build immunity
- Popular throughout medieval period
- Effectiveness questionable by modern standards

**Specific Antidotes:**
- **Belladonna antidotes Amanita mushroom poisoning**
- **Hemlock antidotes strychnine poisoning**
- **Milk, charcoal, or binding substances** thought to absorb toxins
- **Many based on folklore and superstition** rather than proven effectiveness

**Poison vs Medicine:**
- **Dose makes the poison:** Same plant therapeutic or lethal depending on amount
- **Deadly nightshade, henbane, mandrake, opium:** All sleep medicines AND poisons
- **Risk:** Incorrect dosing could kill rather than cure

**Source Quality:** Historical records, toxicology research, medieval herbals
**Game Application:** Poison/antidote system, assassination mechanics, risky medicines

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Four Humors Diagnostic System
**Academic Basis:** Hippocrates, Galen, medieval medical theory

**Translation:**
- **Core Concept:**
  - Every person has humoral balance (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile)
  - Illness = imbalance of humors
  - Diagnosis = identify which humor is excess/deficient
  - Treatment = restore balance via herbs, diet, procedures

- **Person Humoral State:**
  ```rust
  struct HumoralBalance {
      blood: f32,       // 0.0-1.0 (ideal ~0.25 each)
      phlegm: f32,
      yellow_bile: f32,
      black_bile: f32,
  }

  impl HumoralBalance {
      fn is_balanced(&self) -> bool {
          let ideal = 0.25;
          let tolerance = 0.10;
          [self.blood, self.phlegm, self.yellow_bile, self.black_bile]
              .iter()
              .all(|&h| (h - ideal).abs() < tolerance)
      }

      fn diagnose_imbalance(&self) -> Option<HumoralImbalance> {
          let mut imbalances = vec![];

          if self.blood > 0.35 {
              imbalances.push((Humor::Blood, "excess"));
          } else if self.blood < 0.15 {
              imbalances.push((Humor::Blood, "deficient"));
          }
          // ... same for other humors

          if !imbalances.is_empty() {
              Some(HumoralImbalance { imbalances })
          } else {
              None
          }
      }
  }
  ```

- **Illness Effects on Humors:**
  ```rust
  enum IllnessType {
      Fever,        // Increases blood (hot & wet)
      Catarrh,      // Increases phlegm (cold & wet)
      Jaundice,     // Increases yellow bile (hot & dry)
      Melancholy,   // Increases black bile (cold & dry)
  }

  fn illness_affects_humors(illness: IllnessType, balance: &mut HumoralBalance) {
      match illness {
          IllnessType::Fever => balance.blood += 0.15,
          IllnessType::Catarrh => balance.phlegm += 0.15,
          IllnessType::Jaundice => balance.yellow_bile += 0.15,
          IllnessType::Melancholy => balance.black_bile += 0.15,
      }
  }
  ```

- **Herbal Treatment (Theory of Opposites):**
  ```rust
  struct Herb {
      name: String,
      properties: HerbalProperties,
      effects: Vec<HumoralEffect>,
  }

  struct HerbalProperties {
      temperature: Temperature,  // Hot, Warm, Neutral, Cool, Cold
      moisture: Moisture,        // Wet, Moist, Neutral, Dry, Arid
  }

  enum Temperature {
      Hot,    // +2
      Warm,   // +1
      Neutral, // 0
      Cool,   // -1
      Cold,   // -2
  }

  // Example herbs:
  let pepper = Herb {
      name: "Black Pepper".to_string(),
      properties: HerbalProperties {
          temperature: Temperature::Hot,
          moisture: Moisture::Dry,
      },
      effects: vec![
          HumoralEffect::Reduce(Humor::Phlegm, 0.10),  // Opposite: phlegm is cold & wet
      ],
  };

  let cucumber = Herb {
      name: "Cucumber".to_string(),
      properties: HerbalProperties {
          temperature: Temperature::Cold,
          moisture: Moisture::Wet,
      },
      effects: vec![
          HumoralEffect::Reduce(Humor::YellowBile, 0.10),  // Opposite: bile is hot & dry
      ],
  };

  fn apply_herbal_remedy(herb: &Herb, balance: &mut HumoralBalance) {
      for effect in &herb.effects {
          match effect {
              HumoralEffect::Reduce(humor, amount) => {
                  match humor {
                      Humor::Blood => balance.blood = (balance.blood - amount).max(0.0),
                      Humor::Phlegm => balance.phlegm = (balance.phlegm - amount).max(0.0),
                      Humor::YellowBile => balance.yellow_bile = (balance.yellow_bile - amount).max(0.0),
                      Humor::BlackBile => balance.black_bile = (balance.black_bile - amount).max(0.0),
                  }
              },
              HumoralEffect::Increase(humor, amount) => {
                  // ... similar
              }
          }
      }

      // Renormalize so they sum to 1.0
      let total = balance.blood + balance.phlegm + balance.yellow_bile + balance.black_bile;
      balance.blood /= total;
      balance.phlegm /= total;
      balance.yellow_bile /= total;
      balance.black_bile /= total;
  }
  ```

**Educational Value:** Teaches medieval medical theory, historical worldview

**Fun Factor:** Diagnostic puzzle, matching remedies to symptoms

**Implementation:** Humoral balance stat, herb property matching system

  fn harvest_herb(plot: &mut HerbPlot) -> HarvestResult {
      if plot.growth_stage != GrowthStage::HarvestReady {
          return HarvestResult::NotReady;
      }

      let base_yield = 10;
      let quality_modifier = plot.health;
      let yield_amount = (base_yield as f32 * quality_modifier) as u32;

      plot.growth_stage = GrowthStage::Seedling;  // Reset
      plot.days_until_harvest = plot.herb.unwrap().growth_time();

      HarvestResult::Success {
          herb: plot.herb.unwrap(),
          quantity: yield_amount,
          quality: plot.health,
      }
  }

  fn dry_herbs(fresh_herbs: Vec<Herb>, drying_rack: &DryingRack) -> Vec<DriedHerb> {
      // Drying preserves for 1 year
      fresh_herbs.into_iter().map(|h| DriedHerb {
          herb_type: h.herb_type,
          potency: h.potency * 0.9,  // Slight potency loss from drying
          expiration: current_date() + 365,
      }).collect()
  }
  ```

  fn forage_for_herbs(location: Location, season: Season, herbalism_skill: f32) -> ForageResult {
      let available_herbs: Vec<&WildHerb> = WILD_HERBS
          .iter()
          .filter(|h| h.locations.contains(&location) && h.seasonal_availability.contains(&season))
          .collect();

      if available_herbs.is_empty() {
          return ForageResult::NothingFound;

      // Weighted by rarity
      let herb = select_weighted_random(&available_herbs);

      // Identification check
      let identification_chance = herbalism_skill - herb.identification_difficulty + 0.3;

      if rng.gen::<f32>() < identification_chance {
          // Successfully identified
          let quantity = roll_quantity(herb.rarity);
          ForageResult::Found {
              herb: herb.herb_type,
              quantity,
              correctly_identified: true,
          }
      } else {
          // Misidentified - might pick poisonous plant by mistake!
          if herb.herb_type.is_poisonous() && rng.gen::<f32>() < 0.3 {
              ForageResult::Found {
                  herb: HerbType::Hemlock,  // Dangerous mistake!
                  quantity: 1,
                  correctly_identified: false,  // Player doesn't know!
              }
          } else {
              ForageResult::NothingFound
          }
      }
  }
  ```

**Educational Value:** Teaches wild plant identification risks

**Fun Factor:** Exploration, seasonal gameplay, risk of poisonous misidentification

**Implementation:** Location-based foraging with skill checks

- **Poisoning Mechanic:**
  ```rust
  fn apply_poison(person: &mut Person, poison: PoisonEffect) {
      person.status_effects.push(StatusEffect::Poisoned {
          poison: poison.poison,
          time_remaining: poison.onset_time + poison.duration,
          stage: PoisonStage::Onset,
      });
  }

  fn update_poisoned_person(person: &mut Person, dt: f32) {
      for effect in &mut person.status_effects {
          if let StatusEffect::Poisoned { poison, time_remaining, stage } = effect {
              *time_remaining -= dt as u32;

              match stage {
                  PoisonStage::Onset if *time_remaining < poison.duration => {
                      // Symptoms appear
                      *stage = PoisonStage::Symptomatic;
                      apply_symptoms(person, poison.get_symptoms());
                  },
                  PoisonStage::Symptomatic if *time_remaining < 30 => {
                      // Terminal stage
                      *stage = PoisonStage::Terminal;
                      // Death roll
                      if rng.gen::<f32>() < poison.get_lethality() {
                          person.alive = false;
                      }
                  },
                  _ => {}
              }
          }
      }
  }
  ```

- **Antidote System:**
  ```rust
  struct Antidote {
      name: String,
      effective_against: Vec<Poison>,
      effectiveness: f32,  // 0.0-1.0
      preparation: Recipe,
  }

  // Mithridatium (universal antidote)
  let mithridatium = Antidote {
      name: "Mithridatium".to_string(),
      effective_against: vec![
          Poison::Hemlock,
          Poison::Belladonna,
          Poison::Mandrake,
          Poison::Henbane,
      ],
      effectiveness: 0.4,  // Only moderately effective (historical accuracy)
      preparation: complex_recipe_with_dozens_of_ingredients(),
  };

  // Specific antidote (belladonna for mushrooms)
  let belladonna_antidote = Antidote {
      name: "Belladonna Extract".to_string(),
      effective_against: vec![Poison::AmanitaMushroom],
      effectiveness: 0.7,  // More effective for specific poison
      preparation: simple_tincture_recipe(),
  };

  fn apply_antidote(person: &mut Person, antidote: &Antidote) -> AntidoteResult {
      for effect in &mut person.status_effects {
          if let StatusEffect::Poisoned { poison, stage, .. } = effect {
              if antidote.effective_against.contains(poison) {
                  // Success chance based on effectiveness and stage
                  let success_chance = match stage {
                      PoisonStage::Onset => antidote.effectiveness,
                      PoisonStage::Symptomatic => antidote.effectiveness * 0.7,
                      PoisonStage::Terminal => antidote.effectiveness * 0.3,
                  };

                  if rng.gen::<f32>() < success_chance {
                      *effect = StatusEffect::None;
                      return AntidoteResult::Cured;
                  } else {
                      return AntidoteResult::Ineffective;
                  }
              }
          }
      }

      AntidoteResult::NoPoison
  }
  ```

- **Poison Usage (Assassination, Warfare):**
  - Players can craft poisons
  - Apply to food, drink, weapons
  - Risk: Accidental self-poisoning
  - Detection: Taste testers, poison immunity (mithridatism)

**Educational Value:** Teaches dual nature of medicinal plants, poison history

**Fun Factor:** Assassination plots, antidote races, risky medicine

**Implementation:** Status effect system with antidote matching

## Domain Translation Patterns

### Pattern: Medical Theory → Game Stats
**Approach:**
1. Four humors map to balance stats (0.0-1.0 each)
2. Illness shifts humoral balance
3. Herbs restore balance via Theory of Opposites
4. Diagnosis = identify imbalance

**Example:**
```
# Historical: Fever = excess blood (hot & wet)
# Game: fever illness increases blood stat to 0.40
# Treatment: Apply cold/dry herb (cucumber) to reduce blood
```

---

### Pattern: Preparation Methods → Crafting Mechanics
**Approach:**
1. Each historical method becomes recipe type
2. Different tools, times, difficulties
3. Success depends on skill + recipe difficulty
4. Quality affects remedy potency

**Example:**
```
# Historical: Decoction = boil roots/bark for 20-60 min
# Game: Decoction recipe requires pot, 20 min craft time, moderate difficulty
```

---

### Pattern: Herb Properties → Item Database
**Approach:**
1. Historical herbs become items with properties
2. Hot/cold, wet/dry encoded as stats
3. Ailments treated mapped to game illnesses
4. Rarity, growth time from historical sources

**Example:**
```
# Historical: Willow bark treats fever, contains salicin
# Game: Willow Bark item, hot property, treats fever illness, 45 day growth
```
