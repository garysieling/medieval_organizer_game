# Literature Review: Comparative Religions — Structural Dimensions of Faith

**Sprint:** 6 — Comparative Religion (targeted research; no implementation)
**Focus:** the world's religious traditions as *combinations of structural axioms* — how descent-vs-mission membership, orthopraxy-vs-orthodoxy, law-vs-ethics, and exclusive-vs-accommodative truth claims generate both religions AND their conflicts.
**Date:** 2026-10-25

## The framing insight (what the research actually says)

The standard comparative-religion move is to treat each faith as a monolith. The better literature treats religion as a *vector over shared dimensions* — which is exactly what the game needs. Three references anchor the method:

- **Ninian Smart — Dimensions of the Sacred / The Religious Experience of Mankind:** the canonical dimensional taxonomy — ritual, narrative/mythic, experiential/emotional, social/institutional, ethical/legal, doctrinal/philosophical, material (buildings/objects). A "religion" is a profile across these, not a membership card.
- **Ernst Troeltsch + Rodney Stark — church/sect typology:** religions exist on a tension gradient with society — *church* (accommodated, world-accepting, born-into) vs *sect* (tension, voluntary, demanding). Stark's work on conversion (The Rise of Christianity; One True God) shows conversion happens mostly along *social networks*, not doctrine — people convert through kin and friends, keeping prior ties. And his sect-cycle: sects form in tension → soften into churches → spawn new sects. A generative schism engine.
- **Norman Cohn — The Pursuit of the Millennium (1957):** medieval millenarianism — apocalyptic expectation among the poor as *revolutionary* fuel. The poor's religion is not the church's religion; under stress it ignites into mass movements (the Flagellants, the Free Spirit, Münster). The direct precursor of the revolt-theology mechanics.

## The traditions — structural profiles

### Judaism (the descent-covenant archetype)
- **Membership:** primarily descent — matrilineal by rabbinic law + covenant (brit milah). Conversion exists but is *discouraged and costly* — Judaism is a people with a religion, not a recruiting religion. (Early rabbinic Judaism had more mission activity; post-70 it became boundary-strict.)
- **Truth claim:** covenantal — chosen people, not universal truth for all. Other nations have the Noahide laws (basic decency), not the Torah — **a religion that doesn't think everyone should join it.** Structurally unique.
- **Orthopraxy-dominant:** halakha is a *total life code* — food, dress, calendar, sex, business, prayer times. What you *do* matters more than what you *believe*; theological diversity is tolerated inside the practice.
- **Sacred text:** Torah + Talmud — a law-and-commentary tradition, endlessly interpretable; learning is worship.
- **Afterlife:** weakly specified historically (Sheol); resurrection enters late. Earthly covenant-keeping is the point.
- **Authority:** rabbinic (scholar-judge), post-Temple diasporic — a *portable* religion; law replaces land (the key adaptation after exile).
- **Conflict signature:** boundary defense — under pressure, the response is *tighter practice*, not violence; pogroms produce martyrdom/communal endurance, not crusades.

### Christianity (the mission-universal archetype, and its many streams)
- **Membership:** universal mission — "all nations." Entry by baptism/initiation, not descent. Paul demolished the boundary rules (no circumcision/kosher) to make mass conversion possible — *the design change that created Christianity*.
- **Truth claim:** exclusive + supersessionist — the one true path AND the fulfillment that supersedes Judaism; the brother-relationship creates the sharpest possible boundary problem (the People God Chose vs the people who think God moved on).
- **Orthodoxy-dominant (anomalously so):** Christianity is the odd religion that runs on *correct belief* — creeds, councils, heresy trials. Most religions don't care much what you believe; Christianity made belief the boundary — hence *heresy* as a category (unique-ish to Christianity/Islam).
- **Streams:** Catholic (sacramental, hierarchy, saints), Orthodox (liturgical, autocephalous), the medieval heresies (Cathars — dualist, anti-matter; Waldensians — lay preaching, poverty), then the Reformations. Each is a different axiom remix within the family.
- **Sacred text:** Bible — inspired + canon; authority filtered through church for medieval Catholics.
- **Afterlife:** central — resurrection, judgment, heaven/hell/purgatory; the economy of salvation (indulgences, masses for the dead) is *load-bearing* medieval economics.
- **Authority:** priestly hierarchy with real worldly power (the medieval church was the largest landholder); celibate clergy (an axiom most religions don't pick).
- **Saints/relics (Peter Brown — The Cult of the Saints, 1981):** the medieval *practice* was a patron-shrine-relic religion — saints as heavenly patrons you could petition, whose bones were *power sources*. Towns competed for relics; pilgrimage was the economy. Brown is essential: official Christianity ≠ practiced Christianity.
- **Folk Christianity (Aaron Gurevich — Medieval Popular Culture, 1988):** the peasantry ran a *different* Christianity — agrarian magic, weather rites, the dead as neighbors, pagan substrate under Christian vocabulary. Priests managed rather than eliminated this.

### Islam (the law-and-mission universalist)
- **Membership:** universal mission (dawah) — simple entry (shahada). Like Christianity, missionizing; unlike it, orthopraxy-dominant.
- **Truth claim:** supersessionist toward both — the final correction of Judaism and Christianity; strictest monotheism (any partner-assignment to God is shirk, the one unforgivable sin).
- **Orthopraxy-dominant:** sharia as total life code — prayer 5x/day, fasting, alms, pilgrimage, dietary law, family law, commerce law. Law, not creed, is the spine.
- **Sacred text:** Quran as literal divine speech — the most verbatim-text axiom of any major faith; the recitation itself is sacred act.
- **Streams:** Sunni (community + tradition), Shia (lineage of Ali, martyrdom theology — the Karbala myth is a revolt-shaped sacred story), Sufi (mystical, initiatory orders — the main medieval mass-conversion vector).
- **Authority:** no priesthood in the Christian sense — ulama (scholar-jurists) interpret law; the caliph is political, not pontifical.
- **Church-state:** no separation concept — but the dhimmi system (protected-but-taxed minorities) is a *codified* interfaith-coexistence axiom most religions lack; it let Judaism/Christianity persist as parallel law communities (the millet model).
- **Afterlife:** judgment + paradise/hell, vivid and central.

### Hinduism (the descent-polytheist-accommodative archetype)
- **Membership:** birth/descent — you're born into varna (class/caste); conversion as such barely exists; absorption instead (new deities get *adopted* into the pantheon).
- **Truth claim:** radically accommodative — many paths, many gods, all valid relative to your station; the opposite of exclusive. (Prothero's counter-caution: religions are NOT the same mountain — Hinduism is the extreme "many paths" pole.)
- **Orthopraxy-dominant:** dharma = duty-by-station; caste rules, purity/pollution, life-cycle rites (samskaras). Belief is relatively unbounded; practice is exacting.
- **Sacred text:** layered — Vedas (heard), epics (remembered), Puranas, Bhagavad Gita; oral > written for most of history; priestly transmission (brahmin monopoly on the Veda — hereditary clergy axiom).
- **Afterlife:** samsara — reincarnation through karma; moksha as exit; time is *cyclical* not linear (yugas) — a completely different time-axiom than Abrahamic linear-eschatology.
- **Authority:** brahmin hereditary priesthood + renouncer traditions (guru, sannyasi) — the ascetic is a parallel authority *outside* the priesthood.
- **Streams:** bhakti devotion (personal god), philosophical schools (Vedanta — monist: all is Brahman), folk/local deity worship — the same religion is simultaneously polytheist, monist, and devotional.

### Buddhism (the path-not-god archetype)
- **Membership:** voluntary + mission (Ashoka's missions, monastic orders spreading along trade routes) — converts by practice, not descent.
- **Truth claim:** the path works — not a god-claim; early Buddhism is functionally non-theistic (gods exist but can't save you — *they're stuck in samsara too*, which is a delightfully gameable detail).
- **Orthopraxy-ish:** precepts + meditation + monastic rule (vinaya); doctrine matters but as *diagnosis* (suffering→craving→cessation), not creed.
- **Sacred text:** canon of sutras, but transmission through sangha practice.
- **Afterlife:** rebirth/karma like Hinduism, but nirvana = exit by *insight*, not divine grace.
- **Authority:** sangha — monastic community; the monk/lay symbiosis (lay feeds monk, monk generates merit) is a *gift economy* axiom.
- **Streams:** Theravada (orthodox school), Mahayana (bodhisattva ideal — universal salvation, opened to laypeople), Vajrayana (esoteric/initiatory).

### Zoroastrianism (the ancestor-axiom)
- **Membership:** hereditary descent — famously did NOT missionize; the parsis still don't really convert. Its *ideas* spread everywhere while the religion stayed hereditary.
- **Core axioms:** cosmic **dualism** (Ahura Mazda vs Angra Mainyu — good vs evil as real forces), linear-eschatological time, final judgment, heaven/hell, resurrection of the body, fire as purity symbol.
- **The kicker for game design:** Zoroastrianism is probably where Judaism got judgment/afterlife/eschatology during the Babylonian exile — i.e., the Abrahamic afterlife is likely a Zoroastrian *meme* propagated through a *different membership axiom*. Proof that axioms recombine.

### Manichaeism & Gnosticism (the dualist-mystery archetype)
- **Membership:** mystery/sectarian — initiated elect vs hearers (Manichaeism had a literal two-tier laity); missionizing but demand-heavy (Stark's high-tension sect).
- **Core axioms:** dualism radicalized — spirit vs *matter itself* (the body is the prison; the world is evil or a trap); esoteric knowledge as the key (Gnosticism: *gnosis* saves, not faith).
- **Why they died:** demanding + negative-worldview + no demographic replacement; also persecuted by everyone. Stark's data: extreme sects either moderate into churches or burn out — the sect-church lifecycle again.

### Greco-Roman civic religion (the orthopraxic polis)
- **Membership:** civic — you're Roman, you do Roman rites; no conversion concept because *no belief requirement*. Pontiffs read omens; priests were magistrates.
- **Orthopraxy at the maximum:** religion = correct public ritual (pax deorum — keep the gods happy, the state stays lucky); nobody asked what you believed. Augustine's contemporaries literally couldn't conceive of "one true religion" as a category until Christianity invented it.
- **Pantheon as civic bureaucracy:** gods have portfolios; new gods get added by conquest (interpretatio — syncretism as imperial tech).

### Folk/shamanic substrate (the baseline)
- **Membership:** territorial/tribal — your people, your spirits.
- **Core axioms:** spirit-world is real and local (ancestors, place-spirits, weather powers); imagistic ritual (Whitehouse) — rare intense rites, initiations, ecstatics; specialists part-time (shaman = practitioner, not priest); no text, no doctrine, pure practice.
- **Persistence:** this layer *never dies* — it gets relabeled under every formal religion (the saints absorb the spirits; the feast day absorbs the solstice rite). `dantes-divine-comedy.md` and Gurevich both point at this.

## Cross-cutting dimensions (where the axioms come from)

1. **Membership mode:** descent (matri-/patrilineal) / mission-universal / territorial-civic / initiatory-mystery / covenant-chosen — *the biggest axiom; drives convert-flow, border conflict, and "who counts"*
2. **Practice-vs-belief dominance:** orthopraxy (correct conduct; Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Roman) vs orthodoxy (correct belief; Christianity, Islam partly, Gnosis)
3. **Truth stance:** exclusive / supersessionist / accommodative-syncretic / parallel-valid (your gods for you, mine for me — the default ancient stance)
4. **Law scope:** total-life-code / ritual-law-only / ethics-only
5. **God-structure:** strict-monotheist / trinitarian / dualist / polytheist-portfolio / monist-all-one / non-theistic-path / spirit-world
6. **Time structure:** linear-eschatological / cyclical-rebirth / seasonal-agricultural
7. **Afterlife:** judgment-resurrection / heaven-hell-purgatory / reincarnation-exit / ancestor-continuation / underworld-shadow / de-emphasized
8. **Salvation mechanism:** faith/grace / law-keeping / works+faith / enlightenment-insight / devotion-bhakti / initiation-gnosis / none-collective
9. **Authority:** hereditary priest / ordained hierarchy / scholar-jurist / charismatic / monastic-community / magistrate-priest / lay-none
10. **Sacred text:** verbatim-divine / inspired-canon / oral-tradition / none
11. **Ritual mode (Whitehouse):** doctrinal-routine / imagistic-flashpoint / mixed
12. **Religion-state relation:** fused-theocracy / parallel-law-communities / subordinate / anti-political-sect / civic-function
13. **Martyrdom value:** celebrated / accepted / avoided
14. **Icons/images:** forbidden-aniconic / permitted-venerated / neutral
15. **Monasticism:** central / marginal / absent
16. **Women's role:** segregated-restricted / parallel-orders / undifferentiated
17. **Magic stance:** miracles-yes-sorcery-no (the witch-hunt axiom) / magic-as-technique / all-miraculous

## Conflict signatures (axioms → wars)

| Collision | Real example | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive × exclusive | crusades, jihad/counter-jihad | zero-sum truth over shared holy sites |
| Supersessionist × descended | Christian anti-Judaism, pogroms | the "elder brother" tension — can't coexist as equals |
| Missionary × descent | forced conversions, Inquisition | descent-people can't *voluntarily* comply fast enough |
| Orthodox × heretic | Cathar crusade, heresy trials | wrong belief *inside* the faith = pollution worse than another faith |
| Orthopraxic law × foreign rule | dhimmi/millet tension, martyrdoms over pork/idols | law-total faith under a state with different rules |
| Aniconic × iconophile | Byzantine iconoclasm | whether God lives in images = centuries of war |
| Monist/accommodative absorbing exclusive | Hinduism absorbing tribals vs missionaries | a syncretic religion *eats* your gods — the subtler conquest |
| Imagistic × doctrinal | millenarian movements vs the church (Cohn) | the parish can't contain the prophet's bonfire |
| Atheist-path × theist state | Buddhist purges | "your God can't save you" is a fighting claim |

## The medieval Europe particulars (for our world)

- Medieval Christianity was **two religions**: the official sacramental-church one (doctrine, canon law, sacraments, Latin) and the folk-village one (saints, shrines, wells, weather-rites, the dead — Gurevich, Brown). Both are real; the gap between them is a mechanic (a village can be *Christian on paper, pagan in practice*).
- Cohn's millenarianism: when subsistence breaks + a prophet appears + the poor have lost the moral economy → apocalyptic movements that torch the social order. Religion isn't just the ruler's glue — it's also the *revolt's theology*.
- Judaic communities = the medieval dhimmi-style parallel-law people — tolerated-by-charter, legally separate, protected and exploited; a real axiom configuration, not an edge case.
- The parish was the *organizational unit* of village life (Blickle's communes again) — church, village assembly, poor relief, feast calendar, all in one institution.
