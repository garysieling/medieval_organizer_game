## Executive Summary

The round-2 corpus left four sources un-surveyed. Each maps to a
mechanic the game already half-implements: the spycops inquiry to the
existing provocateur/plant machinery; INCITE!'s NGO-ization critique to
the paternalism/institutions layer; DFRLab's platform-manipulation to

**Confidence Level:** MEDIUM (secondary scholarship, no archive work)

## 1. UK Spycops (Undercover Policing Inquiry)

The richest modern infiltration record: officers lived inside activist
groups for years, fathered children under cover, and wrote intelligence
that shaped the state's response. The pattern's a controlled
opposition - the plant who becomes indistinguishable from the real
member.

**Game Mechanic Proposal:** a long-tenure `Plant` escalation - the
plant who's embedded past a threshold (`Organizing`/`grievances` over
time) becomes a `Turned` member (the cover's own cost: the plant who
stays too long starts believing it). The reveal's the inquiry's own
arc: a delayed `exposed` event that costs the state its legitimacy AND
the org its trust - the members who learned they were spied on carry
a deeper grievance than a simple crackdown produces.

## 2. INCITE! - "The Revolution Will Not Be Funded"

The NGO-ization critique: professional movement organizations that
depend on foundation funding drift away from the communities they
organized - the staff's own survival becomes the mission, the risky
work defunded.

**Game Mechanic Proposal:** the `institutions`/`locally_sponsored`
contrast made legible - a sponsored org (funded by the power, per the
`incorporation_offer`) has a lower-risk `agitation` ceiling (the
funding's own constraint: the org that takes the power's money can't
run the power's own fight). The mechanic's the INCITE! argument: the
sponsored org's capacity grows but its `absolutism`/`separation` drifts
toward the sponsor - co-option as a soft ceiling, not a hard stop.

## 3. DFRLab - Platform Manipulation Research

The modern information-ops scholarship: coordinated inauthentic
behavior, the astroturfed "grassroots" campaign, the manufactured
consensus that reads as organic.

**Game Mechanic Proposal:** the `Watcher`'s own astroturf counter -
the power manufactures a fake "popular" campaign (a `front` org with
high `legitimacy` but no real `members`): the villagers can't tell it
from the real thing until the `front`'s own exposure (the cover's own
cost - the members who joined the fake carry the deepest betrayal).
The game's own lesson: legibility's the counter - the org that CAN
prove its members are real (the `petition_count`/`members` roll)
outlasts the manufactured one.

## 4. Color-Revolution Rollback Scholarship

The authoritarian-learning literature: regimes studied the Color
Revolutions and built the counter-playbook - pre-emptive arrests,
NGO law, controlled opposition, the legal frame that makes organizing
illegal before it's effective.

**Game Mechanic Proposal:** the `inquisition`/`administrative
attrition`/`compliance burden` machinery's own escalation - the power
LEARNS from a near-loss (`OrganizedPower.capacity` past a threshold):
the counter-playbook's own adoption (the `Watcher.paranoia` + the
`lawfare`/`repression_debt` up-shifts - the power that almost lost
writes the law that makes the next rising illegal). The game's own
thesis: the power's not static - it learns, and the second rising's
harder than the first.

---

## Synthesis

The four sources converge on the game's own thesis: the organizing's
not a one-time rising but a learning loop - the power adapts (spycops,
the counter-playbook, the funding leash, the manufactured fake), and
the org's own answer is the legibility, the real members, the
self-organization that survives the counter-movement. Each proposal
wires the existing machinery (plant, institutions, front, inquisition)
into the escalation the sources describe.
