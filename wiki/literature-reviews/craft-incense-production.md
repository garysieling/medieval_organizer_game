# Lit Review: Incense — Sacred Smoke as Trade, Craft & Sensory Theology

**Survey Date:** 2026-10-31
**Agent:** Literature Survey Agent
**Domain:** Medieval Craft, Long-Distance Trade, Liturgical Economics
**Series:** Deep-dive crafts (user-requested; complements [sprint-7-perfume-and-smell.md](sprint-7-perfume-and-smell.md) which covers the odor vector but not the supply chain)
**Confidence:** High — Pliny, medieval recipes, and the archaeology are all documented

## Purpose

Incense is the perfect medieval trade+craft+religion case: a luxury commodity that *burns* (consumed on use, perpetual demand), produced at the far edge of the known world, monopolized by intermediary civilizations, and converted into liturgical meaning by the church. The supply chain spans a continent; the consumption is a 30-second censer swing. Every part is gameable.

## Part 1: The Resins (the raw materials)

| Resin | Source | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frankincense** (olibanum) | *Boswellia* trees — southern Arabia (Dhofar), Somalia | The church incense; highest status | Tapped resin, dried to "tears"; grading by size/color; the tree only grows in arid zones |
| **Myrrh** | *Commiphora* — same regions | Co-equal prestige (the Magi pair) | Bitter, darker; also medicine/embalming |
| **Benzoin** | *Styrax* — SE Asia | Later medieval import | Vanilla-adjacent sweetness |
| **Copal/storax/labdanum** | Americas/regional | Mid-tier substitutes | Regional incense economies |
| **Pine resin/mastic/local pitch** | Local Mediterranean/north | **The poor parish tier** | The crucial substitute — every village church burned *something* |

The tiering matters: frankincense was the gold standard, but the medieval world ran on *substitutes* — a village priest burned pine resin and called it incense. Scent theology didn't require the import; it required the *smoke*. That's the craft-economy wedge: same ritual, different cost.

## Part 2: The Incense Road (the distribution machine)

- **South Arabia → Petra → Mediterranean**: the frankincense trail — ~2,000 miles of caravan route through Nabataean territory, who took tolls on every stage and deliberately obscured the source (the **source-mystification monopoly**: the Nabataeans spread tales of dragon-guarded groves so buyers couldn't bypass them — information asymmetry as trade policy, the earliest documented version of protecting a supply chain).
- **The price multiplication**: resin worth little at the Dhofar grove reached Rome/Constantinople at luxury prices — Pliny complained about the empire's silver drain on aromatics (the ancient trade-deficit grumble; incense+spices+silk are all in his list). High value-to-weight + long distance = the caravan good *par excellence*.
- **The medieval hand-off**: Arab-controlled Red Sea routes → Alexandria/Constantinople → Venice & Genoa as the European distribution chokepoints → spice/apothecary guilds in each city → parish/household. Each hop is a guild, a toll, a profit layer — incense is a *stack of middlemen* before it's a smell.
- **The Sabaean precedent**: the incense kingdoms (Sheba/Saba) built whole states on the resin monopoly — a *resource* theocracy before the word existed.

## Part 3: The Craft (making the burnable thing)

- **Grains/tears**: raw resin burned directly on hot charcoal — the simplest form; the skill is in the *blend* (frankincense + storax + benzoin + aromatics — documented church-incense recipes)
- **Compounding**: pastilles, grain-incense mixtures, sweetened blends — the apothecary/spicer as manufacturer (incense lived in the same trade as medicine and spices — one shop sold all three because the supply chain was one)
- **The delivery tech**: charcoal disks/pucks as the ignition layer; self-burning compounds (cones/sticks) need an oxidizer — *saltpeter* — meaning modern incense tech is literally gunpowder-adjacent (the medieval version stayed charcoal-on-brazier, which is why the thurible exists)
- **Thuribles**: the swinging censer as engineered ritual object — chains, balance, draft control. The apex is **Santiago's Botafumeiro** (documented since 11th c.): a ~80kg silver censer swung on a 20m pulley arc through the cathedral transept by a team of tiraboleiros — liturgical engineering as spectacle; it exists partly to mask the smell of unwashed pilgrims (functional and ceremonial at once — the medieval crowd-control deodorant).

## Part 4: The Theology of Smoke

- **Psalm 141**: "let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense" — the smoke IS the prayer made visible; the sensory theology is not decoration, it's doctrine (visible ascent = acceptable prayer)
- **Liturgical grammar**: censing the altar, the book, the congregation, the dead — the censer *marks the sacred zones*; High Mass vs Low partly *defined* by the censer
- **Sensory boundary**: incense smell marks church-space from street-space (crossing the threshold = crossing the scent boundary — odor as architecture; connects to the perfume doc's odor vector)
- **The Reformation rejection**: incense = "popery's sensory propaganda" — the Puritans stripped it as doctrinal marker (smell as theology, again: to remove the smell is to change the doctrine). Scent was *confessionally marked* — you could tell a High Anglican church from a Puritan chapel by nose
- **The counter-church of smell**: Orthodoxy *increased* it — the liturgical arms race in vapor

## Part 5: The Economics of Sacred Consumption

- **Perpetual demand**: burned on use — the church is a *consumer*, not a collector; the congregation's piety is literally fuel
- **Status display**: how much incense you burn = how important the mass is (frankincense for the high altar, pine for the side chapel — the ritual budget as status economy)
- **Monastic self-supply**: richer houses could stock their own (through monastic trade routes); poorer parishes depended on the town spicer — a **consumption dependency** that makes the church a customer of the long-distance trade it preached against luxury for
- **Gift and offering**: frankincense as gift-good (the Magi aren't casual — it's diplomatic-grade tribute); the resin moves through gift networks as well as market ones

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: The incense supply chain (trade-good)
`resin` source zones (arid southern regions) → caravan stages → coastal entrepôts → spice guilds → parish/household consumption. Per-hop markup + toll mechanics; source-mystification stat on supplier factions (their info asymmetry = their margin). Disruption events: route blocked → prices spike → substitute surge (poor parishes already on pine; rich ones feel the pinch).

### Mechanic 2: The substitute-tier system
Incense has `prestige` tiers (frankincense > regional resins > pine/pitch). Ritual quality scales with tier, but the *sacramental function* doesn't — the church accepts pine. Player/world tradeoff: status spending vs. functional worship — the ritual budget as a real economy stat.

### Mechanic 3: Scent as space-marker
Church `odor_radius` — incense establishes sacred-space boundaries in the smell layer (ties to perfume doc's odor vectors). Higher-tier incense = wider radius + status signal; rival confessions' scents mark territory (the Reformation scent-war mechanic).

### Mechanic 4: Thurible/spectacle objects
`liturgical_engineering` objects — censers, the Botafumeiro-class spectacle censer (construction + crew + maintenance): public liturgical spectacle events that boost `perceived_power`/piety of the sponsoring church and draw pilgrims (spectacle buildings as faith-infrastructure — pairs with relic mechanics).

### Mechanic 5: Consumption dependency
Churches have `incense_demand` (perpetual — burns each mass); supply interruption degrades ritual quality → congregation morale/piety dips. This makes the spice trade *load-bearing infrastructure* for the faith sim — the invisible dependency that connects the parish to the caravan.
