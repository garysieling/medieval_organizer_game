# Lit Review - Sprint 3: Community Organizing Tactics

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Sociology / Community Organizing
**Sources:** 4 works (Alinsky tradition, task groups, relational meetings)

## Sources

### 1. Tufts Civic Studies - Community Organizing
- Standard tactics: 1-1 relational interviews, house meetings, strategic discussions, direct actions
- **Finding:** Organizing strengthens people's capacity to work together and exercise power
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Our core theme. 1-1s, house meetings, power building.

### 2. Schutz & Sandy - Collective Action for Social Change
- Organizer vs leader distinction: organizers do day-to-day work + train leaders; leaders govern + provide public face
- **Finding:** Groups are democratic but decisions ratify small leader group choices
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Two roles in our community sim: organizer (recruits/trains) vs leader (governs/decides)

### 3. NEA - Fundamentals of Organizing (Relational Meeting guide)
- Detailed 1-1 structure: build relationship → identify concerns → identify networks → evaluate leadership → agitate → create action
- **Finding:** "Strategic vulnerability" + curiosity + empathy; meeting is planned non-task dialogue
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - Exact mechanic for our 1-1 conversations. Identifies networks + leadership potential.

### 4. Staples - Community Organizing for Social Justice
- Task-oriented groups as participation structure
- **Finding:** Organizing = collective action drawing on numbers + participatory processes + indigenous leadership to decrease power disparities
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Power disparities theme; task groups (issue committees, recruitment teams) as mechanics.

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **1-1 Relational Meeting** - the core organizing move:
   - Purpose: build relationship, identify concerns/networks/leadership, agitate, create action
   - Mechanic: player/organizer talks to citizen → reveals info (concerns, connections, potential) → deepens bond → recruits
   - Output: trust + info + a recruited contact

2. **House Meetings** - small group gathering of identified people:
   - Goal: build relationships + social capital + develop agenda
   - Mechanic: gather 5-10 identified citizens → group bonding → shared concerns emerge → form committee

3. **Organizer vs Leader** - two distinct roles:
   - Organizer: does work, trains leaders, behind scenes
   - Leader: governs, decides, public face
   - Mechanic: player starts as organizer, must develop leaders (can't do everything)

4. **Task Groups** - committees for specific functions:
   - Issue committees, recruitment, governance, fundraising, media
   - Mechanic: form committees around issues; each produces capability

5. **Power Building** - influence without authority → actual authority:
   - Numbers + participatory process + indigenous leadership → decreased power disparity
   - Mechanic: grow membership + train leaders + win small victories → accumulate power

## Design Proposals

- **1-1 system**: talk to citizen → reveal their concern + network + leadership potential → build trust → invite to meeting
- **Leadership pipeline**: identify potential leaders → mentor them → they lead their own group
- **Committee formation**: gather interested members → assign issue → committee produces resources/influence
- **Campaigns**: pick target → build pressure → extract concession → grow power

## Rating
**Critical** - Directly models our core game theme. 1-1s, house meetings, organizer/leader roles, and power building are all concrete mechanics.
