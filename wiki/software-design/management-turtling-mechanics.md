# Management / Turtling Mechanics

**Category:** Design / Strategy
**Source:** Backlog item (High) — "Management/turtling mechanics: defense-focused gameplay."
**Date:** 2026-10-25

## Description

The defensive/management play style — how the game supports players who want to *hunker down and build well* rather than expand. Different from `strategic-flexibility-chokepoints.md` (the map/strategy view): this is about the *mechanics* of fortification, self-sufficiency, and depth-oriented play.

## What turtling means mechanically

- **Depth over breadth** — a small settlement developed intensively: better buildings, higher skills, deeper infrastructure — the village-as-garden play style
- **Fortification** — walls, gates, watchtowers, moats; a fortified position raises the cost of attack (siege becomes a starvation race, `non-rps-combat.md`)
- **Self-sufficiency** — closed-loop production: food, tools, clothing, building materials made locally; trade becomes optional, not required
- **Reserves** — granaries, stockpiles, water: the turtle's real weapon is stored surplus; a siege or famine is a reserve-management problem
- **Redundancy** — a turtle builds spare capacity: two wells, multiple food sources, fallback supplies — resilience *is* the strategy

## The turtle's economy

- Import-substitution rather than export-led — the turtle pays more (local production is less efficient than trade) but is *immune* to trade disruption
- Storage as a mechanic — granaries, cellars, drying/smoking; preservation tech (salt, smoke, fermentation) is a real tech line for turtles
- The turtle's vulnerability is *internal*: a single disaster (fire, plague, crop failure) without redundancy is existential — so redundancy is the actual skill

## Design principles

- **Turtling must have a counter-force** — pure isolation is boring; the world should push back: raids probe walls, a siege tests reserves, diplomacy pressures isolation. Turtling is a *response* to pressure, not a safe mode
- **Defense as delay** — a turtle wins by making attacks cost more than the prize; the goal is to be *not worth attacking*, not unbeatable
- **Fortification costs the turtle too** — walls are expensive in labor/materials; building a fortress means not building a mill — defense is a resource allocation tradeoff, not a free shield
- **The turtle's stories are domestic** — the fun isn't the siege (rare), it's the thriving small place: festivals, crafts, the village *living well* while empires burn around it (fits `medieval-thematic-research.md` texture)
