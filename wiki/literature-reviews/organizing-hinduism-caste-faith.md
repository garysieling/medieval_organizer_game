## Purpose

Caste is the world's longest-running experiment in **hereditary social stratification sacralized by religion** — and Hindu history is simultaneously the strongest evidence that the *same* religion can legitimate the hierarchy AND fuel its internal subversion. This doc covers caste as a system, the bhakti counter-movements (the anti-gatekeeping devotional tradition), the missionary dynamic (liberation + exploitation + backlash), and the modern Dalit organizing that turned exit itself into a mass-movement tactic. Maps directly onto the game's stratification system (`sim/stratification.rs`) and the faith-power interface.

## Part 1: The Caste Machine (sociology)

**Two layers:**
- **Varna** — the fourfold scriptural schema (Brahmin-priest, Kshatriya-warrior, Vaishya-merchant, Shudra-laborer + the "varna-less" outcastes/untouchables): ideology of cosmic order, hereditary purity, and occupational duty (dharma as assigned station).
- **Jati** — the ~3,000 real hereditary endogamous occupational groups people actually live in: marriage circles, guild-like occupational lock-in, commensality rules (who can eat/drink with whom), local hierarchy.

**The mechanics that make it durable:**
- **Purity/pollution economics** — ritual status attaches to occupation (tanners, sweepers at the bottom); the "unclean" work is *assigned* by birth, and the assignment is the pollution.
- **Sanskritization** (Srinivas) — lower castes can climb by imitating upper-caste practice (adopt deities, rituals, vegetarianism) — individual mobility that *reinforces the frame* (you move up by accepting the game, not abolishing it).
- **The Brahmin monopoly on interpretation** — scripture access gated through a hereditary literate class; ritual specialists as gatekeepers (the ultimate broker class — see faith doc).
- **Temple economies** — temples as redistributive/economic institutions with hereditary service castes attached (priests, dancers, artisans) — religion + labor + land fused.

## Part 2: The Bhakti Counter-Tradition (internal subversion)

Hinduism's own recurring anti-gatekeeping movement — vernacular devotion that bypasses Brahmin mediation:

- **Bhakti movements (c. 800–1700)**: poet-saints singing in vernacular languages (not Sanskrit) that devotion is direct, unmediated, and open to all castes and genders. Basava (12th c. Lingayat — founded a caste-rejecting order; still a distinct tradition), Kabir (weaver, attacked both Hindu and Muslim gatekeeping), Ravidas/Raidas (cobbler saint — a Chamar whose verses entered the Sikh canon), Mirabai (woman saint who refused the role), Chaitanya (ecstatic mass devotion, Krishna movement).
- **The structural pattern**: identical to Lollards/Waldensians — vernacularization breaks the clerical monopoly; devotion-in-the-mother-tongue is counter-organizing in ecclesiastical form. The bhakti poet-saints are organic intellectuals two centuries before Gramsci named the type.
- **Limits**: bhakti rarely dismantled caste — it created devotional equality inside a still-caste society (Lingayats developed their own hierarchy). The pattern: subversion within the frame, not abolition of it (sect-formation as pressure-release — Niebuhr's drift again).

## Part 3: The Missionary Dynamic (liberation + exploitation + backlash)

The user-requested case: missionaries "liberating" low-caste people — with the exploitation dimension.

**The mass-conversion phenomenon (1860s–1930s, documented):**
- Protestant missions grew Indian Christianity from ~1M (1860) to ~5M (1930) — **~80% of converts came through *group/mass movements*, not individual conversion.** Whole kin groups, jati clusters, village sections converted together (Shanars of Tirunelveli, Chuhras of Punjab, Telugu Malas/Madigas during famines, Mizos).
- **The mechanism**: conversion as *collective caste-escape* — baptism into a faith that (at least formally) rejected caste hierarchy, plus mission schools/employers as alternative institutions. The mission offered the "respectable shield" and institutional anchoring to communities with neither. It was organizing delivered as religion.
- **Missionary surprise**: the missionaries hadn't planned mass-movement conversions — the movement was *led by Dalit initiative* (Pickett 1933 and modern scholarship) — the converts organized their own group conversion; the missions followed.
- **The dependency problem**: "rice Christians" accusation — famine-linked conversions entangled spiritual liberation with material dependency; the mission became patron (land, food, jobs) and the convert became client — a comprador-adjacent dynamic running *downward* into the poorest.
- **The exploitation & hypocrisy layer** (the user's sexual-exploitation thread, abstracted): colonial "rescue" discourse (Spivak's canonical frame: "white men saving brown women from brown men") justified intervention while British power was itself implicated in exploitation — most documented case being the **devadasi debates**: hereditary temple servants sexually exploited *by* the temple/elite system; the colonial anti-nautch campaign criminalized them while British officials patronized the same system — the conflict the user mentioned (missionary moral campaigns vs colonial practice vs actual victims' interests — the three-way that never resolves cleanly).
- **The backlash**: missionary success + conversion produced Hindu counter-organizing — the Arya Samaj (1875) invented *shuddhi* (re-conversion) missions to reclaim converts, the Sanghathan (organization) movement to build Hindu institutional infrastructure to match the missions' — the counter-movement forming to meet the threat (exactly like the Dominicans being founded to out-preach heresy).

## Part 4: Modern Dalit Organizing (exit-as-movement)

- **Jyotirao Phule** (1827–90): Satyashodhak Samaj (Truth-Seekers' Society, 1873) — first mass non-Brahmin movement; schools for untouchables and girls (with Savitribai Phule — the first woman teacher); *Gulamgiri* ("Slavery") framing caste *as* slavery — the abolitionist analogy made native.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** (1891–1956): Dalit jurist, constitution-drafter, mass organizer — temple-entry campaigns, the Mahad satyagraha (1927 — water-tank access claim with mass burnings of the Manusmriti), labor organizing, electoral politics.
- **The ultimate exit**: in 1956 Ambedkar led ~400,000–500,000 Dalits in **mass conversion to Navayana Buddhism** — abandoning Hinduism *collectively* as a political act. Exit not as personal choice but as mass-movement tactic — the largest organized faith-defection in history. "I was born a Hindu, but I will not die a Hindu."
- **Pattern**: the oppressed don't just resist the hierarchy — they *leave it*, en masse, when an alternative faith-structure offers dignity + institutions. Conversion is a *vote with your soul* — and the mass version is a campaign.

## The Cross-Cutting Patterns

1. **Sacralized hierarchy is the most durable** — caste outlasted every political regime because it lives in ritual, marriage, and occupation, not just law (a stratification system with religious enforcement is nearly indestructible)
2. **The gatekeeper monopoly is the strategic target** — every counter-movement (bhakti, missions, Buddhism, Lingayats, Sikhism) first attacked the Brahmin monopoly on the sacred — *interpretation access IS the battleground*
3. **Mass conversion = collective defection** — kin/jati groups move together (networked defection, not individual choice) — the same "structure test" logic: solidarity converts at scale or not at all
4. **Liberation institutions carry their own exploitation** — mission paternalism, devadasi hypocrisy, "rescue" discourse weaponizing victimhood — the intermediary role is *always* ambiguous (faith doc confirmed again)
5. **Counter-movements get absorbed into the frame** — bhakti sects develop hierarchies; Lingayats develop castes; converts find discrimination follows them (Dalit Christians still face caste inside the church — documented) — the frame outlasts the escape attempt
6. **Deity cults as org infrastructure** — bhakti traditions, temple networks, pilgrimage circuits are durable movement scaffolding (the IAF-congregation pattern, 1000 years early)

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Hereditary-stratification layer (for `stratification.rs`)
Jati-like hereditary groups with `ritual_status` + `occupation_lock` — status separate from wealth/power (a poor Brahmin outranks a rich Shudra). Mobility = `sanskritization` (imitate upward) — individual move that reinforces the system (great emergent trap: mobility *strengthens* stratification).

### Mechanic 2: Interpretation monopoly as lever
`scripture_access` stat per faction — gatekeeping class (Brahmin) profits from monopoly; counter-movements can run vernacularization (lay preaching, translation) — the bhakti/Lollard move: each vernacularization event weakens the gatekeeper's legitimacy and raises the constituency's `critical_agency` (ties to pedagogy doc).

### Mechanic 3: Mass conversion as campaign type
`mass_defection` events: whole kin/jati groups convert when (a) an alternative institution offers dignity+material support, (b) grievance crosses threshold, (c) a movement structure exists to coordinate the jump. Player-facing: build the landing institution first (school, mutual aid, employment) — conversion follows infrastructure.

### Mechanic 4: Exploitation-embedded liberation institutions
"Rescue" institutions carry `ambiguity` — they deliver real uplift *and* extraction (mission schools educating + patronizing; the broker role again). The faction receiving them can flip (Dalit converts → Dalit Christian theology with its own critique inside the host institution — documented) — the institution becomes contested terrain, not just an asset.

### Mechanic 5: Exit-as-tactic
Community can run collective-defection campaigns (Ambedkar's mass conversion, Remença-style migration, maroon flight): mass exit removes the constituency the hierarchy extracts from — the ultimate nonviolent sanction. Cost: all the social capital of the abandoned network. Win condition for oppressed factions: make exit cheap enough to be credible (the Palmares/mission-school/Buddhist-sangha landing infrastructure).

### Mechanic 6: Devadasi-type exploitation dynamic (abstracted, no sexual content)
Hereditary service castes bound to institutions with exploitation hidden as "dedication" — a legitimacy liability: exposure events turn institutional piety into scandal (the anti-nautch campaign's actual leverage). Keep abstract: `hereditary_servitude` institution type producing exploitation-risk events.
