# Lit Review - Sprint 4: Medieval Social Stratification & Power Dynamics

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Medieval History / Social Hierarchy
**Sources:** Labour and Lordship, Medieval Peasants, Regional Framework, Medieval England

## Sources

### 1. Labour and Lordship (Oxford)
- **Regional diversity + change** were hallmarks of early medieval hierarchies
- **Free vs unfree:** legally important but social reality more complex
- **Land control = power:** inequalities organized around who controls land
- **Multiple lordships:** different forms touched peasants' lives
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - power = land control, multiple authority types

### 2. Medieval Peasants: Any Lessons? (JPS)
- **Surplus extraction:** peasants' product above subsistence appropriated by landowners (direct/church/state)
- **Village hierarchy was economic** not age/sex-determined
- **Peasants not autonomous:** linked via markets to towns, clergy, nobles
- **Conflict over surplus:** disputes about rents/services, serfdom, jurisdiction
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - class struggle over surplus, village hierarchy is economic

### 3. Regional Framework: Power, Property, Social Structures (Oxford)
- **Fertile regions -> large manors** (king/religious institutions)
- **Infertile -> small landowners** remained
- **Coastal regions -> no manorialism, freedom + self-determination**
- **Rise of villages/towns/guilds/commons:** dissolution of central power enabled self-organization
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - geography + power dissolution -> self-organization emergence

### 4. Medieval England: Rural Society (1086-1348)
- **Gross inequalities:** mass of peasants vs distinguished few (theologians, builders)
- **Stone buildings rare:** ordinary houses wood/daub, impermanent
- **Economic disparities omnipresent**
- **Relevance:** HIGH - inequality is the medieval baseline

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **Power = land control** - who controls land has authority
2. **Multiple lordships** - different authority types affect peasants
3. **Surplus extraction** - peasants' product above subsistence taken by landowners
4. **Village hierarchy is economic** - wealth determines rank, not age/sex
5. **Geography determines organization** - fertile->manors, coastal->free towns
6. **Power dissolution -> self-organization** - when central power weakens, villages/guilds form
7. **Conflict over surplus** - peasants vs lords over rents/services (class struggle)
8. **Gross inequality is baseline** - mass vs few, ordinary vs exceptional

## Design Proposals

- **Land control = power** - authority flows from who controls resources
- **Multiple authorities** - different lord types (secular/religious/merchant) affect villagers
- **Surplus extraction** - villages produce, lords extract (class tension)
- **Economic hierarchy** - village rank by wealth, not birth
- **Geography matters** - terrain determines settlement type (fertile->manor, coast->free)
- **Dissolution -> organization** - when lords weaken, self-organizing emerges (guilds, villages)
- **Class conflict** - peasants vs lords over surplus (recurring tension)
- **Inequality baseline** - mass of workers vs few elite

## Rating
**Critical** - Medieval power dynamics directly model our vision: land control = authority, multiple lord types, surplus extraction (class tension), economic hierarchy, dissolution -> self-organization. The village is where these forces play out.
