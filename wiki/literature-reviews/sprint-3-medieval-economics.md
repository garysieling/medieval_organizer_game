# Lit Review - Sprint 3: Medieval Markets & Guild Economics

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Economics / Medieval History
**Sources:** 4 academic works

## Sources

### 1. "Hunger and Market Dynamics in Pre-modern Communities" (2007)
- Multi-agent model of medieval urban economy during food shortages
- **Finding:** Market intervention (price controls) can reduce famine effects but must be goal-directed and complex
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Directly applicable to our market + hunger systems. Food crises + policy effects.

### 2. "Merchant Guilds, Taxation and Social Capital" (CEPR)
- Theory of guild emergence as cooperation mechanism between merchants and rulers
- **Finding:** Guilds enforce trade monopoly via social capital; explains variation in guild behavior
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Guild mechanics for our faction system. Social capital = relationship power.

### 3. "Coordination, Commitment, Enforcement: Merchant Guild" (JPE)
- Greif/Milgrom/Weingast - repeated game model of guilds
- **Finding:** Guilds emerged to let rulers commit to merchant security; secured property rights
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Contracts + enforcement theme. Guilds as institutions that make contracts credible.

### 4. "Modelling Maritime Trade Systems" (ABM of Hanseatic trade)
- Agent-based model of medieval long-distance trade
- **Finding:** Hanseatic network emerged from geography, transport costs, trade privileges, info asymmetries
- **Relevance:** HIGH - Trade network emergence, geographic factors in trade patterns

## Key Insights

1. **Guilds = credible commitment** - they let rulers/merchants make believable promises (our contract system)
2. **Food crises are policy tests** - market intervention has real, modeled effects (our economy + hunger)
3. **Trade networks are emergent** - geography + costs + privileges produce network structure (our factions/trade)
4. **Social capital is real** - guild power comes from relationships, not just wealth (our influence/community systems)

## Mechanics to Extract

- **Guild formation** - agents spontaneously organize when trade is risky
- **Famine events** - food shortages + policy interventions produce varied outcomes
- **Trade privileges** - grants/taxes shape which routes get used
- **Information asymmetry** - different agents know different things (affects trade)

## Rating
**High** - 4 strong sources on medieval econ with clear mechanical translations.
