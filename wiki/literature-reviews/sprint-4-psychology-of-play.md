# Lit Review - Sprint 4: Psychology of Play / Flow

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Game Design / Player Psychology
**Sources:** Flow synthesis framework, meta-analysis of flow in games, scoping review

## Sources

### 1. Flow in gaming (IJISAM 2014)
- **Flow definition:** optimal experience, completely absorbed, lose track of time, nothing else matters
- **3 key conditions:** clear goals, unambiguous feedback, balance of challenge and skill
- **Dimensions:** control, narrowed focus, immersion
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - our game needs these for "fun_first"

### 2. Flow in video games: meta-analysis (43 studies)
- **Design elements that influence flow:** information input/output techniques, in-game content, multiplayer aspects
- **Video games ideal for flow:** clear goals, immediate feedback, manipulable difficulty
- **Relevance:** HIGH - concrete design elements to build in

### 3. Scoping Review of Flow Research (252 studies)
- **3-level framework:** Individual (personality, motivation, emotion), Contextual (interindividual factors), Cultural
- **Interactivity and playfulness positively associated with flow**
- **Relevance:** HIGH - flow isn't just mechanics; personality/context/culture matter

### 4. Building blocks for enjoyable games (IJHCS 2021)
- **Systematic review of what makes games fun**
- **Relevance:** HIGH - concrete building blocks

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **Clear goals** - player knows what to do (not ambiguous)
2. **Unambiguous feedback** - every action produces visible response
3. **Challenge/skill balance** - not too easy (boring), not too hard (frustrating)
4. **Interactivity** - meaningful choices, not passive
5. **Control** - player feels agency, not scripted

## Design Proposals

- **Goals:** clear objectives each session (build X, recruit Y, reach Z)
- **Feedback:** immediate visible response to every action (numbers, renders, effects)
- **Difficulty:** adapt challenge to player skill (auto-balance)
- **Choice:** real decisions with consequences, not click-through
- **Progress:** visible advancement (influence, organization, tech)
- **Flow state:** absorbing, time-loss gameplay = the target

## What Makes Our Game Fun (from these)

- **Clear goals:** build your organization, gain influence
- **Feedback:** see org grow, villagers react, world change
- **Challenge:** managing resources, recruiting, organizing
- **Interactivity:** talk/recruit/invest/organize choices matter
- **Control:** you're building, not just watching

## Rating
**Critical** - Flow psychology validates our "fun_first" approach: clear goals, feedback, challenge/skill balance, interactivity, control. These are measurable design targets.
