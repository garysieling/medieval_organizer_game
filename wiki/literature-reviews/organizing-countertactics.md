## Purpose

The mirror of the campaigns survey: **how power fights back against organizers**. This is the best-documented area of movement history because the FBI's own files became public record. Covers: COINTELPRO tactics, private-sector union-busting, controlled opposition (company unions, astroturf), agent provocateurs, false flags / manufactured pretexts, divide-and-rule, and — critically — **what movements learned to do about it** (counter-countertactics).

## Part 1: The Documented Tactics

### A. COINTELPRO (FBI, 1956–1971) — the canonical case
Source: Church Committee (US Senate, 1975–76), Books II–III — 2,370 approved covert actions over 15 years, exposed only because activists (Citizens' Commission to Investigate the FBI) broke into the Media, PA field office in March 1971 and leaked documents to the press.

**Tactic taxonomy from the Committee's findings:**

| Tactic | Documented example | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Snitch-jacketing** | William Albertson (CP-USA leader expelled after FBI forged an informant report naming him an informant) | Destroy a member's trustworthiness; the org does the expelling itself |
| **Provocateur infiltration** | Informants "seized every opportunity to carry out disruptive activity," pushed divisive motions, sabotaged plans, urged violence | Degrade meetings, waste resources, produce arrests |
| **Fomenting lethal conflict** | Anonymous forged letters inflaming Black Panther Party vs US Organization feud → shootings (Bunchy Carter & John Huggins killed at UCLA, 1969) | Let rival factions destroy each other |
| **Personal-life attacks** | Anonymous letters to spouses alleging infidelity (used against MLK and others); efforts to get targets fired; IRS referrals | Attrition through demoralization and job loss |
| **Assassination-enabling** | FBI informant William O'Neal provided the floor plan used in the 1969 police raid that killed Fred Hampton | Decapitation of leadership |
| **Psychological warfare** | The MLK "suicide letter" (1964): FBI package urging King to kill himself before his Nobel ceremony; fake sex-tape threats | Break the leader |
| **Reputation poisoning** | Planted false gossip that Jean Seberg (BPP supporter) was pregnant by a Panther → her suicide 1979; fake BPP "coloring book" mailed to white suburbs to scare off donors | Cut off allies and funding |
| **Counterfeit literature** | Bureau-authored newsletters and leaflets attributed to movements | Control the movement's message; provoke splits |
| **Media manipulation** | Reporters fed derogatory info; favorable coverage suppressed | Frame the movement before it frames itself |
| **Coordination with local police** | Pre-arranged arrests on pretext during actions | Escalate repression while keeping federal hands clean |

**Key finding for mechanics:** most COINTELPRO damage was not raids — it was **trust destruction**. The memo logic of snitch-jacketing was that the *suspicion itself* is the weapon: movements tore themselves apart checking for infiltrators. The immune response does the work.

### B. Private-sector counter-organizing (the corporate playbook, 1870s–present)

**Pinkerton & the detective agencies (1870s–1930s):**
- Infiltrated unions at scale (James McParland inside the Molly Maguires — infiltrated, testified, hanged 10 men; contested how much he fabricated).
- Strikebreaking: Homestead 1892 — armed Pinkertons fought a pitched battle with strikers; Coeur d'Alene, Pullman.
- The FBI's political-repression toolbox was *transferred wholesale* from the private detective agencies into the federal state in the WWI–1920s transition (Smith & McIntosh / published research on "Pinkerton to G-Man" continuity — same tactics, new letterhead).

**The American Plan & Mohawk Valley Formula (1920s–30s):**
- Employers' "open shop" campaigns dressed as civic freedom.
- **Mohawk Valley Formula** (documented employer playbook, named by the NLRB in the 1936 Remington Rand strike): a 9-step recipe — 1) brand unionists as outside agitators, 2) form a "citizens' committee" of local elites to demand order, 3) declare law-and-order emergency, 4) deputize/organize vigilante force, 5) stage a "back to work" march of loyal workers, 6) escalate to get state police/guard involved, 7) break the strike under color of restoring peace. This is a literal **step-by-step counter-organizing algorithm** — directly translatable to a game AI playbook for power-holders.
- **Company unions / Employee Representation Plans** (1913–1935): employer-funded "unions" that give workers a voice-without-power — the canonical **controlled opposition**. So effective at absorbing organizing energy that the Wagner Act (1935) *banned* them outright. Still the model for "union avoidance" today ($400M+/yr industry; captive-audience meetings, front groups).

**Modern forms:** union-avoidance consulting (documented scripts: captive-audience meetings, supervisor training, delay-and-litigate NLRB elections); SLAPP suits; economic reprisal (firing lead organizers — illegal but cheap at the penalty rates); "astroturf" front groups (fake grassroots — tobacco's smokers' rights orgs, climate denial front groups, telecom "consumer" groups).

### C. Controlled opposition — the deep pattern
Power creates or captures the *appearance* of opposition to absorb real opposition:

1. **Company unions** (above) — sanctioned voice channel that can't strike.
2. **Bantustan/collaborationist leadership** (apartheid SA): homeland leaders, Inkatha — and crucially, the state *secretly funded* Inkatha's anti-ANC violence ("Inkathagate," exposed 1991) — a state creating a "rival movement" to bleed the real one.
3. **Colonial indirect rule**: governing through co-opted chiefs/intermediaries who take the blame and split resistance.
4. **Co-option of moderate wings**: regimes elevate the "reasonable" faction to isolate militants (then repress the militants) — standard colonial and Cold War practice; also corporate "partnership" NGOs that take funding to moderate demands.
5. **Astroturf orgs**: industry-built fake publics to simulate counter-mobilization — the perception weapon Alinsky warned about, run in reverse.

The common structure: **manufacture legitimacy for the counter-position so the status quo never has to defend itself directly.**

### D. Agent provocateurs — converting discipline into legitimacy for repression
- **Labor era**: infiltrated radicals urging violence gave cover for mass arrests (documented across Molly Maguires, Wobblies cases).
- **COINTELPRO-era**: informants routinely *proposed* violence and supplied the means (documented in Church Committee; informant-provocateurs in the BPP and anti-war movements; e.g., informants who led activists into federal charges while the Bureau shielded them).
- **Modern policing**: UK's undercover policing scandal — officers like Mark Kennedy lived 7+ years inside environmental movements, fathering relationships, while feeding intelligence; some infiltrators acted as provocateurs at protests (public inquiry ongoing — the "spycops" scandal).
- **Strategic function**: a movement's greatest asset is nonviolent discipline (political jiu-jitsu). The provocateur's job is to **break discipline once, publicly**, so repression becomes legitimate. One molotov outweighs ten thousand marchers.

### E. False flags & manufactured pretexts (documented cases)
- **Mukden 1931**: Japanese army staged a railway explosion to justify invading Manchuria (confirmed).
- **Gleiwitz 1939**: Nazi operatives in Polish uniforms "attacked" a German radio station to justify invading Poland (confirmed at Nuremberg).
- **Shelling of Mainila 1939**: USSR shelled its own village, blamed Finland, invaded (confirmed by Soviet archives).
- **Gulf of Tonkin 1964**: the second "attack" on US ships almost certainly never happened (declassified NSA docs, 2005) — used to pass the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution.
- **Reichstag Fire 1933**: whoever set it, the Nazi regime *instantly* exploited it for the Decree suspending civil liberties — the template of **crisis exploitation**: the flag doesn't even need to be false if you can ride a real incident.
- **COINTELPRO fake documents**: forged letters attributing violence/plans to movement figures = intra-movement false flags.

The pattern for gameplay: **manufactured crisis → emergency powers → repression with legitimacy.** Movements that can't document/refute fast enough lose the narrative window.

### F. Divide-and-rule & decapitation
- Foment factionalism (BPP–US; Newton–Cleaver rift engineered partly by forged letters — documented).
- Legal attrition: injunctions (Pullman strike killed by injunction, 1894), endless litigation, conspiracy charges against leadership (Chicago 8).
- Assassination/elimination: Fred Hampton, Bunchy Carter, MLK, Malcolm X, Steve Biko, Imam Haron; death squads in Latin America — the record is long and partly state-linked.
- **Decapitation works best against charismatic-bottleneck orgs** — which is why leadership pipelines matter (see campaigns doc, Mechanic 5).

### G. Soft repression (underrated, probably most effective)
- **Reform absorption**: grant a diluted version of the demand to demobilize (partial concessions timed to peak pressure — Bismarck's social insurance to gut socialists; apartheid's "reform" era tricameral parliament that split the opposition by *partially* including some).
- **Respectability leverage**: invite leaders into elite spaces, fund them, give status — the NGO-ization critique (INCITE!, *The Revolution Will Not Be Funded*).
- **Bureaucratization**: channel protest into hearings/consultations where it dies by procedure.
- **Legality asymmetry**: selective enforcement — permits denied, noise ordinances, "unlawful assembly" declared at discretion.
- **Flooding the zone**: media saturation so movement framing can't surface (see H).

### H. Information war
- Planting stories, buying columnists (documented FBI practice), "outside agitator" framing, crime-ification (protest → "riot" relabel), astroturf opinion, and modern equivalents (bots, coordinated inauthentic amplification — documented in recent platform transparency reports).
- Historical media counter: movements ran **their own press** — *Liberator*, *Freedom's Journal*, movement papers, later indymedia — because the mainstream wouldn't carry the frame.

## Part 2: Counter-Countertactics — What Movements Learned

This is the part that matters for the game: organized communities developed **real defensive doctrine**.

1. **Assume infiltration; design for it.** Open organizations can't be infiltrated in a damaging way — there's nothing secret to steal. Cell structure trades recruitment speed for resilience (Otpor; ANC underground). Player-facing tradeoff.
2. **Vetting through relationships.** The 1-1 relational meeting is *also* a security tool: you vouch for people you actually know. IAF's relational discipline is quietly counter-infiltration infrastructure.
3. **Nonviolence discipline + marshal training.** Trained peacekeepers whose job is to *isolate provocateurs* — a formal role in SNCC/anti-war marches. A march with discipline enforcement is a different unit than a march without.
4. **"Verify, don't accuse."** Norms against rumor-based purges are the direct counter to snitch-jacketing — because the jacket only works if the org's immune system does the expelling.
5. **Exploit their surveillance.** Birmingham organizers knew phones were tapped and **fed false info through the taps** to misdirect police (documented in the Birmingham campaign record) — turning surveillance into a disinformation channel for the movement. Beautiful mechanic candidate.
6. **Distributed leadership.** SNCC/Ella Baker's group-centered model, Otpor's cells, UDF's decentralized fronts — decapitation-resistant by design.
7. **Documentation & counter-intelligence.** The Media, PA break-in (1971) — activists doing counter-intel on the state, which is what *ended* COINTELPRO. Whistleblowing and records access as movement functions (FOIA as weapon).
8. **Cross-movement solidarity as armor.** Black Panthers fed the disability sit-in at HEW (1977) — ally networks provide logistics when your org is under siege; isolated orgs die faster.
9. **Independent press + direct media access.** Own channel to public = can't be de-platformed by one newspaper's framing.
10. **Legal infrastructure.** Movement lawyering (NLG), bail funds, knowing rights — attrition defense, not just defense.

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Repression tech tree (power-holder side)
Escalating tools, each with cost/risk: **Surveillance** (intel gain, low cost) → **Infiltration** (intel + sabotage options; risk of exposure scandal) → **Legal harassment** (attrition; cheap, deniable) → **Provocation** (attempts to break movement discipline → legitimizes heavy repression if it works; massive legitimacy loss if caught) → **Violent repression** (fast suppression; backfire check — see jiu-jitsu mechanic in campaigns doc). Medieval equivalents already exist: informers, agent provocateur = hired rabble-rouser, legal harassment = selective fines/summons, violent repression = the guard.

### Mechanic 2: Trust/paranoia as a separate stat
Infiltration + snitch-jacketing events raise **paranoia**; paranoia itself degrades org function (slower decisions, purge-risk events, recruitment penalty) — modeling COINTELPRO's actual mechanism. Counter-stats: `verification_norms` (built via 1-1 density, slows rumor-purges), `transparency` (open orgs take less infiltration damage but leak more intel).

### Mechanic 3: Controlled opposition events
Power-holder offers a seat at the table via a captive institution (works council, advisory board, council seat). Payoff for power-holder: movement energy absorbed. Detection mini-game: is the seat **binding** (concession real, enforceable) or **performative** (advice only, no teeth)? Accepting performative seats costs reputation with the base; refusing real ones loses free wins. Company-union history makes this a real dilemma, not a fake choice.

### Mechanic 4: Provocateur events
Infiltrated orgs generate provocation events (member proposes violent action at the worst moment). Counter options: trained marshals (discipline stat), quick internal process (verify-then-act), or eject-on-suspicion (cheap but feeds paranoia and can hit innocents — the snitch-jacket trap).

### Mechanic 5: Manufactured crisis
Power-holders can trigger or exploit incidents (real or staged) to declare emergency measures — suspending assemblies, banning gatherings, purging suspects. Movement counters: documentation capacity (witnesses → refutation window), independent media (frame contest), elite allies (denial of legitimacy). The refutation **window** is the timed mechanic: expose the flag fast and it's a scandal; too slow and repression is already normalized.

### Mechanic 6: Mohawk Valley playbook as counter-AI
Power-holding factions run the documented 9-step sequence as an actual scripted strategy: delegitimize → citizens' committee → emergency → vigilante force → "back to work" theatre → state force → break. Players learn to interrupt the sequence early (contest the "agitator" frame, split the citizens' committee, document vigilantism) rather than fight the endgame.

### Mechanic 7: Counter-intel as player capability
Documentation, records, whistleblowers, infiltrator-detection (relational vetting), and surveillance-exploitation (feed false info through known taps) as unlockable org abilities — the Media PA break-in as the ultimate counter-intel action: high risk, potential to *end* the repression program entirely via exposure.
