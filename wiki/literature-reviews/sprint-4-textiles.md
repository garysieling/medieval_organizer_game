# Lit Review - Sprint 4: Textiles/Fabric History

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Economic History / Material Culture
**Sources:** Munro (wool economy), Oxbow (textiles + economy), textile production

## Sources

### 1. Munro - Wool and Wool-Based Textiles (UT-ECIPA-MUNRO-00-05)
- **Wool = essential input:** 60-75% of luxury fabric cost, plus oils/fullers earth/teasels/dyestuffs
- **Production chain:** sorting -> combing -> carding -> spinning -> weaving -> fulling/felting -> teaseling -> shearing -> dyeing -> finishing
- **Quality tiers:** cheap (2 weeks wages) -> ultra-luxury scarlets (years of mason's wages, rivaling silk)
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - wool is the medieval textile economy; production chain maps to our economy system

### 2. Oxbow - Textiles and the Medieval Economy (8th-16th c)
- **International trade:** Scandinavia, England, Iceland all linked via cloth trade
- **Luxury for everyone?:** silk embroidery on shoes reached middle classes
- **Social implications:** cloth quality signaled status; imports drove commercial wealth
- **Relevance:** HIGH - textiles were THE international trade good; status + wealth

### 3. Excavations - Textiles and clothing c.1150-1450
- **Types:** wool, goathair, linen, silk, mixed cloths, narrow wares
- **Techniques:** sewing, dyeing, finishing
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM - variety of fibers + products

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **Wool is king** - 60-75% of luxury fabric cost; the input that drives the whole economy
2. **Multi-step production** - sorting->combing->carding->spinning->weaving->fulling->finishing (7+ steps)
3. **Quality tiers** - cheap stuffs -> scarlets (huge price range, same base fiber)
4. **International trade** - cloth linked distant economies (Scandinavia/Iceland/England)
5. **Status signal** - what you wear shows rank; even shoes get silk embroidery
6. **Sensory/texture** - wool (rough) vs silk (smooth) vs linen vs hemp - different comfort levels

## Design Proposals

- **Textile production chain:** wool->spin->weave->finish (uses our recipes/producers)
- **Fiber types:** wool (cheap, rough), linen (common, medium), silk (luxury, smooth), hemp (coarse)
- **Quality economics:** same fiber, different skill -> cheap vs scarlet (10x+ price range)
- **Texture sensitivity:** characters with autism spectrum traits get comfort bonuses/penalties by fabric
- **Trade good:** textiles drive international commerce + wealth
- **Status display:** clothing quality affects social standing (rich/poor visible)

## Rating
**Critical** - Textiles were THE medieval trade good. Wool production chain is a perfect economy system, quality tiers enable status mechanics, texture links to sensory/autism design.
