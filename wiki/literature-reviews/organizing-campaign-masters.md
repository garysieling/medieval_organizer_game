**Survey Date:** 2026-10-31
**Agent:** Literature Survey Agent
**Domain:** Social Movement History / Campaign Craft

## Purpose

Round 1 found that wins cluster around *specific, winnable, engineered* campaigns. This survey dissects the four most deliberately engineered campaigns in the record — Salt March (1930), Otpor (1998–2000), ACT UP (1987–96), Section 504 sit-in (1977) — to extract the design grammar: what does a masterfully built campaign actually consist of?

## Case 1: Gandhi's Salt March (1930) — the engineered campaign as art form

**The design:**
- **Target selection**: the salt tax — universal (everyone eats salt), economically trivial to the Raj but symbolically absolute (the state claiming a monopoly on seawater), and *replicable*: any peasant could boil seawater and break the law. Gandhi called salt "the only condiment of the poor."
- **The arc**: 240-mile, 24-day march with 78 hand-picked disciplined marchers — deliberately slow, each village a new audience, international press embedded. Participation grew from 78 → tens of thousands at Dandi. Gandhi boiled salt on a beach on April 6; illegal salt-making swept the coast.
- **The trap**: the Viceroy faced a lose-lose — ignore it (law becomes unenforceable, legitimacy drains) or arrest a 61-year-old man for gathering mud-salt (repression looks absurd). ~60,000–80,000 were jailed anyway, including Gandhi — the jailing itself became the broadcast.
- **Win**: India didn't get independence in 1930 (the march "failed" on immediate terms — Round Table Conferences got him a seat, not swaraj). But it transformed Congress into a mass movement, internationalized the cause, and made salt-tax repeal happen in the interim. The deeper win was *civilizational* — it demonstrated the Raj ruled by consent, and consent could be withdrawn.
- **The discipline lesson**: in 1922, after protesters burned a police station at Chauri Chaura (22 police killed), Gandhi called off the entire non-cooperation movement at its peak — choosing long-run nonviolent credibility over short-run momentum. Costly, controversial, and the reason his campaigns could still trigger political jiu-jitsu in 1930.

**Design grammar extracted:** pick a demand the target *cannot* grant without self-undermining and cannot refuse without self-exposing; build the action as a *narrative arc* (march = time, distance, growing cast, climax); make mass participation trivially cheap; pre-commit to discipline so repression always backfires.

## Case 2: Otpor (Serbia, 1998–2000) — resistance as product design

**The design:**
- **The brand**: a clenched fist, two words ("Otpor" = resistance; "Gotov je" = he's finished), stencilable in seconds — participation was *brand adoption*, not ideological commitment.
- **"Laughtivism"**: deliberately absurd tactics — satirical rallies, a barrel for "arresting" Milošević's effigy, birthday cakes and flowers delivered to police stations. Humor made repression look ridiculous and participation fun (recruitment through delight, not duty).
- **Cell structure + training**: decentralized, consensus-free cells; rigorous nonviolence discipline; members *trained* for arrest (beaten, released, became recruitment evidence — every arrest produced members).
- **Pillar outreach**: Otpor treated police/military as *the audience*, not the enemy — courting them, personalizing them, making defection thinkable. When ordered to fire on crowds in October 2000, security forces defected. Milošević fell in days with ~0 violence.
- **The export**: founders built CANVAS and trained Georgian (Kmara → Rose Revolution 2003), Ukrainian (Pora → Orange Revolution 2004), Egyptian (April 6 → Tahrir 2011) movements. **Critical finding: the playbook worked where the regime couldn't or wouldn't go full-repressive — Belarus (2006+), Iran (2009), and later Egyptian consolidation show it fails against regimes willing to kill at scale and control information.** It's a technology of *exploiting* regime restraint, not overwhelming regime power.
- **Win/loss**: removed Milošević; successors institutionalized unevenly. The export model's deepest legacy may be adversarial — authoritarians studied CANVAS's own playbook and wrote counter-doctrine (see modern counter-playbook doc: foreign-agent laws exist *because of* Otpor's success).

**Design grammar extracted:** lower the cost of joining to near-zero (a sticker); make the opponent's repression look absurd rather than terrifying (humor as armor); train for arrest; design for the security forces' defection explicitly; package the method for export.

## Case 3: ACT UP (1987–1996) — spectacle outside, expertise inside

**The design:**
- **The double structure**: public-facing shock tactics (die-ins at the FDA, Wall Street, St. Patrick's Cathedral; "Silence=Death" pink triangle designed by the Gran Fury collective — propaganda as professional-grade graphic design) combined with the Treatment+Data Committee, which taught itself clinical pharmacology to the point of *drafting trial protocols the FDA adopted*. Outsiders made the noise; insiders became credentialed experts who could negotiate the technical details.
- **Targeting the actual bottleneck**: rather than "awareness," ACT UP mapped the drug-approval pipeline and hit each chokepoint directly — parallel track trials, compassionate use, accelerated approval. The NIH's own director adopted their trial-redesign proposals.
- **Internal tension as fuel**: the org famously fractured (Treatment Action Group split 1992) — expertise-wing vs. direct-action-wing tensions are a recurring movement fission axis (see GLF/GAA in round 1).
- **Wins**: transformed drug approval (accelerated approval pathway, parallel track, patient-community seats on trial boards — now standard). Arguably the most effective patient-policy movement ever: measured in years off drug approval timelines × lives.
- **Loss**: died of its own intensity (burnout, death toll, factional split); the AIDS movement's institutional successors depoliticized.

**Design grammar extracted:** pair spectacle with competence — the target must believe you can hurt them *and* that you're worth negotiating with; map the actual decision pipeline and hit the chokepoint, not the symbol; internal expertise converts rage into negotiable demands.

## Case 4: The 504 Sit-In (April 1977) — the coalition matrix in action

**The design:**
- **The lever**: occupy federal property — HEW regional offices in 10 cities, demanding Secretary Califano sign the Section 504 regulations *unchanged* or resign. Specific, binary, deadline-able.
- **Why SF lasted when others starved**: police in most cities simply starved protesters out. The San Francisco occupation survived **~4 weeks** (April 5–28+) because Kitty Cone and others had spent *years* building a coalition matrix: the Black Panthers delivered hot dinners daily (they knew how to feed an occupation), Glide Memorial Church, labor unions, Mission Rebels and Delancey Street provided food, the Butterfly Brigade smuggled in walkie-talkies, deaf protesters organized sign-language communication through windows after HEW **cut the phone lines** (the famous workaround — occupiers signing to interpreters outside, who relayed to press).
- **The impossible optics**: 150 people with visible disabilities occupying a federal building is unspinnable — every enforcement option (starving them out, dragging them out) was a photo the government couldn't afford. Califano signed the regulations **unchanged** on April 28.
- **The sequel**: the org didn't disband — the coalition rolled straight into the ADA campaign (Capitol Crawl 1990, ADA signed 1990). The 504 sit-in is the rare case where the campaign infrastructure survived its own victory (see enforcement doc).

**Design grammar extracted:** pre-build the logistics coalition before the action (the Panthers' food supply line was the difference between 2 days and 28); choose targets where the power's response options are all losing ones; the most sympathetic occupier + the least sympathetic target = maximum jiu-jitsu; design the sequel before you win.

## The Meta-Pattern: What "Engineered" Means

Across all four, masterful campaigns share a design stack:

| Layer | Salt March | Otpor | ACT UP | 504 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | Symbolic + universal + ungrantable-or-undefendable | "He's finished" (regime removal) | Specific pipeline reforms | Sign the regs, unchanged — binary |
| **Participation cost** | Boil seawater | Stencil a fist | Show up / join a committee | Occupy (high for core, low for supporters) |
| **Media arc** | 24-day march, press embedded | Absurd spectacle engineered daily | Die-in + design shop | Occupation duration itself = drama |
| **Discipline** | Absolute nonviolence, halts on breach | Trained-for-arrest cells | Direct-action wings (looser) | Community-care discipline |
| **Pillar work** | International opinion, Indian masses | Police/military explicitly | Bureaucratic experts (FDA insiders) | Federal workers + public |
| **Logistics** | Route, food, salt | Funding (US/int'l — the later vulnerability) | Kitchen/table infrastructure | Panthers' food line, walkie-talkies |
| **Counter-proofing** | Discipline → arrests backfire | Humor → repression looks absurd | Expertise → can't be dismissed | Optics → enforcement unaffordable |

**The universal design rule**: every master campaign is built so that *the opponent's best available response still advances the movement*. That is the deepest formulation of political jiu-jitsu — not just reacting to repression, but architecting the situation so all branches of the decision tree favor you.

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Campaign design phases
Campaigns get explicit design stages: **demand selection** (diffuse/specific; symbolic/economic), **lever selection** (what dependency can you actually squeeze), **participation design** (cost to join), **discipline investment**, **logistics build** (food/comm/legal — the underrated win factor), **pillar outreach**. Master campaigns score on all six; weak campaigns fail on whichever they skipped.

### Mechanic 2: The decision-tree rule
Score actions by "does the opponent have a non-losing response?" Best tactics force all-branches-lose situations (arrest = backfire, ignore = law unenforced, negotiate = concede). This can be a literal evaluation heuristic for both player design and AI counter-play.

### Mechanic 3: Exportable playbooks
Tactics that succeed can be encoded as playbooks — transferable to other orgs/regions with local modifiers (Otpor→Kmara→Pora). Counter: regimes can *also* study the playbook (counter-adaptation mechanic — each successful use raises the meta-awareness stat of power-holders, reducing the tactic's future effectiveness. Tactical stagnation made literal).

### Mechanic 4: Logistics as a tracked resource
Food supply lines, comms (walkie-talkies/carrier pigeons/bards), legal defense, medical — occupations/strikes consume logistics per turn; the 504-vs-other-cities difference is literally `logistics_supply > duration_needed`.

### Mechanic 5: Discipline as a stat with commitment costs
Nonviolent discipline must be *trained* (member actions) and *maintained* (marshal roles); provocateur events and repression test it. Breaking discipline converts the movement's jiu-jitsu asset into a liability — the single highest-stakes stat in the system.
