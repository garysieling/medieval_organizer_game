# Medieval Thematic Research

**Type:** Thematic Reference / Research Summary
**Source:** Backlog item (High) — "Caesar 2/LotR era stylized aesthetic," authenticity vs stylization decisions.
**Date:** 2026-10-25

## Description

Aesthetic grounding for the medieval world: which period, which region, how much realism vs stylization. The project's stated direction is *Caesar 2 / LotR-era stylized* — i.e., high-medieval-flavor but readable, warm, slightly idealized — not grimdark realism and not generic Tolkien epic.

## Era & region anchors

- **Primary band:** high medieval (c. 1100-1400), Northern/Western European — timber-framed villages, thatch, stone keeps, field systems. This is the era the game's systems (manorial economy, guilds, church, herbology) assume.
- **Secondary flavor:** Mediterranean/Ottoman fringe and Northern trade towns (Hanseatic) for regional variety (see `regional-architectural-variety.md`).

## Visual vocabulary (what "Caesar 2 / LotR era" actually means)

- **Readable isometric massing:** buildings read at a glance — roofline silhouettes carry identity (bakery ≠ smithy ≠ chapel from shape alone).
- **Warm palette:** ochres, moss greens, timber browns, slate blues — saturated but earthy, not neon-fantasy.
- **Scale compression:** villages shown denser and cuter than reality (Caesar-style aggrandizement) — readability beats verisimilitude.
- **Living detail:** smoke, laundry lines, market stalls, animals — the ambient life that made 90s city-builders feel inhabited.

## Authenticity vs stylization decisions

| Choice | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Historical fidelity | *Flavor*, not documentation — use real materials/techniques but don't simulate misery for its own sake |
| Fantasy elements | permitted if systems-grounded (magic sim exists) — but the baseline world reads "historical medieval" |
| Dirt/poverty | present but not grimdark — medieval life was hard *and* colorful; favor colorful |
| UI-era feel | 90s city-builder UI warmth (Caesar 2 vibe): parchment, carved borders, illustrated icons |

## Daily-life texture (the underused source)

The best medieval flavor is *domestic*, not military: guild halls, wash days, harvest festivals, market mornings, saint's-day processions, the apothecary's garden, the scribe's workshop. These map directly onto our systems (guilds, religion, herbology, market, lifecycle) — the aesthetics should come from the systems, not imposed on them.
