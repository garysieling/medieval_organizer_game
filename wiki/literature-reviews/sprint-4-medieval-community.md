# Lit Review - Sprint 4: Medieval Village Community & Social Structure

**Date:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Medieval History / Social Organization
**Sources:** English village community, partnership among peasants, law in village

## Sources

### 1. Village Community & Peasant Society (Oxford)
- **4 approaches:** Institutionalists (legal structures), Social structuralists (cooperation), Marxists (class), Individualists (enterprise)
- **Key insight:** village community defined by both legal frameworks AND cooperative activity
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - community is both formal structure + emergent cooperation

### 2. English Medieval Village Community and Its Decline (JBS)
- **Village community has "shadowy existence"** - scholars debated its importance
- **Peak period around 1300:** villagers cooperated, community organized
- **Post-1350 decline:** unity deteriorated
- **Relevance:** HIGH - communities form, peak, decline (lifecycle)

### 3. Partnership Among Peasants (1270-1520)
- **Partnership = 2+ people pursuing common objectives** (co-operative relationship)
- **Collaborative activities:** joint land, clearing, paid work, bread making, pasture management
- **Partnership breakdown -> litigation** (flurries of disputes)
- **Peace-making:** local courts responded when hostility emerged
- **Resilience:** partnerships contributed to holding resilience (1370-1420)
- **Relevance:** CRITICAL - organizing = forming partnerships, breakdown = conflict

### 4. Law in the Medieval Village Community
- **Law operated differently** - community governance, bye-laws, pledges
- **Bye-laws regulated husbandry** + controlled anti-social behavior
- **Pledges:** guarantors for those taking responsibility
- **Relevance:** HIGH - community governance via rules + social pressure

## Key Mechanics to Extract

1. **Community = legal structure + cooperation** - both formal + emergent
2. **Partnership** - 2+ people, common objectives, mutual co-operation
3. **Partnership breakdown -> litigation** - disputes when collaboration fails
4. **Bye-laws** - community-regulated husbandry + anti-social control
5. **Pledges** - guarantors for responsibilities (social enforcement)
6. **Community lifecycle** - forms, peaks (~1300), declines (~1350)
7. **Peace-making** - courts respond to hostility, restore harmony

## Design Proposals

- **Community as structure + emergence** - legal org + cooperative activity
- **Partnership system** - villagers form partnerships (2+, common goals)
- **Breakdown -> conflict** - failed partnerships produce disputes/litigation
- **Bye-laws** - community rules regulate behavior (social enforcement)
- **Pledges** - villagers vouch for each other's commitments
- **Lifecycle** - communities form, peak, decline (not static)
- **Peace-making** - intervention when hostility emerges

## Rating
**Critical** - Medieval community is exactly what we model: legal structure + cooperative partnership + breakdown conflict + bye-law governance + lifecycle. Organizing = forming partnerships.
