## Purpose

How does a movement's *content* — its actual beliefs — get produced? The user's instincts are historically sharp: creeds are adversarial artifacts (each clause exists because someone argued the opposite), pain was a theological domain *before* it was a medical one, sacraments are membership-assignment technology (which is why they caused wars), and schisms happen by accident when institution-building outruns intent. This doc: the mechanics of belief-formation, then the power politics of who gets to say what the sacraments mean.

## Part 1: Doctrine as Adversarial Boundary-Drawing

**The creed-is-an-answer-document finding (the user's point, confirmed):** read the Apostles'/Nicene creeds as a scorecard of defeated positions — nearly every clause exists to exclude somebody:
- "Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible" — vs. gnostic/dualist creation-denial
- "Born of the virgin Mary" — vs. docetists who said Jesus only *seemed* human
- "Suffered under Pontius Pilate" — pins the event to real history vs. mythicist/spiritual readings
- "The resurrection of the body" — vs. dualist flesh-denial
- "The holy catholic church" — vs. sectarian claims to be the only true one
- Nicene's "homoousios" (same substance) — a single Greek word invented at Nicaea 325 specifically to exclude Arius's position; the entire fourth-century crisis was over one letter (homoiousios, "similar," was the compromise the orthodox rejected)

**The production mechanism**: councils are dispute-resolution events — doctrine gets *written at the meeting*, not discovered beforehand. Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381, Chalcedon 451, Trent (18 years of sessions, 1545–63, defining everything the Reformation contested — counter-doctrine as institution-building). Each council's output is a negotiated settlement: the text is literally the ceasefire.

**Heresiology as genre**: orthodoxy is defined by contrast — the heresiologists (Irenaeus, Epiphanius, Bernard Gui later) *wrote the catalogs* of what's excluded, and in writing them often created the taxonomy (groups got named and defined by their enemies — "Waldensian," "Anabaptist," "Quaker" are all originally hostile labels worn later as badges — the same naming-dynamics as modern movement labels).

**Two competing epistemologies**: scholasticism (belief by disputation — the university method, doctrine provable by logic) vs. mysticism (belief by direct experience — Eckhart, Julian, Marguerite Porete *burned* for her book 1310). The institution policed the boundary between them — mystics were tolerated if they submitted experience to doctrine, eliminated if they claimed the experience sufficed (the sacred-science / doctrine-over-person pattern, centuries early).

## Part 2: The Theology of Pain (pre-medicine)

**The church ran the pain-management infrastructure before anesthetics existed** — this is the deepest version of the "what did religion actually do" question:

- **Redemptive suffering**: pain as participation in Christ's suffering (imitatio Christi) — the stigmata devotion, voluntary asceticism, the "celebration of pain" the game's religion-economics loop already models (per the KB note: economic scarcity → religious coping, celebration of pain, apocalyptic hope)
- **Theology of affliction**: suffering explained *theologically* (trial, punishment, purgation, divine pedagogy) — the church supplied the *meaning framework* for an experience with no material remedy
- **Sacraments as palliative tech**: last rites, anointing of the sick, relics and shrines as pain/illness infrastructure (the medieval hospital was itself a religious institution — hospitality, not medicine, was its model)
- **Purgatory**: a doctrine that made even death negotiable — suffering-as-currency; the doctrine's abuse (selling relief from it — indulgences) is what kicked off the Reformation

**The disruption case — anesthesia (1847–53)**: when Simpson introduced chloroform for childbirth, clergy *opposed* it — Genesis "in sorrow shalt thou bring forth children" was doctrine, not metaphor; pain was theologically assigned. The taboo broke when Queen Victoria used it (1853, then 1857) — elite defection (the pillar-pull in miniature: the monarch's body as the opinion leader). **Pattern: when technology removes the need the theology explained, the doctrine itself becomes contested** — the religion-economics feedback loop the KB already tracks (tech removes need → crisis of faith → reorganization).

## Part 3: Sacraments as Membership-Assignment Technology

**The key reframe (user's instinct, confirmed):** the sacramental controversies were wars over **who assigns membership in the community** — which is sovereignty, which is why states cared, which is why people died:

- **Baptism = citizenship enrollment**: infant baptism made every birth a state-church registration — Christendom's census and passport in one rite. **That's why believers' (adult) baptism was a capital crime**: rebaptism wasn't a theological quibble, it was refusal of the state-assigned identity — Anabaptists were drowned (the mocking "third baptism") in Zürich and elsewhere for exactly this reason. The sacrament was the ID system; changing it was sedition.
- **Communion = membership enforcement**: the table as boundary device — who may eat, who's excluded (excommunication as social death), *how* they eat (Hussite both-kinds demand = symbolic claim of lay equality against clerical monopoly — the chalice as the movement's flag, literally borne on banners).
- **Host desecration accusations** — documented pogrom technology: alleged stolen/profaned hosts (Brussels 1370, dozens of cases) → massacres; the sacred object as manufactured-crisis trigger (false-flag family, see countertactics doc).
- **The Book of Common Prayer rebellions**: imposed liturgy as sovereignty claim — Cornwall rose 1549 demanding the Latin mass back ("we will not receive the new service because it is but like a Christmas game"); Scotland 1637 riot over the imposed prayer book (the stool-throwing at St. Giles → Covenanter movement → civil war). Uniformity laws = the state mandating the *form* of worship; the form IS the point.
- **Excommunication/interdict as statecraft**: Innocent III's interdict on England (1208–13) suspended *all* sacraments kingdom-wide — no baptisms, burials, masses — until John surrendered England as a papal fief. The church could shut down the *entire* spiritual infrastructure of a country as leverage. Sacrament supply as siege weapon.

## Part 4: State vs. Church — the Sovereignty Contest

- **Cuius regio, eius religio** (Augsburg 1555): the ruler assigns the religion — the state capturing the church *legally*; Westphalia confirmed it as the European model
- **Act of Supremacy (1534)**: Henry VIII made himself head of the church — state-building via ecclesiastical coup; dissolution of the monasteries as the largest property transfer in English history (the real prize was always partly fiscal — church wealth as conquest target)
- **Gallicanism/Josephinism**: kings limiting papal interference — the proto-national church
- **Kulturkampf** (Bismarck vs. the Catholic church, 1870s): the state's open attempt to break an international church — *failed*; strengthened the Center Party (repression backfires on anchored institutions — the IAF lesson again)
- **Secularization waves**: France's 1790 nationalization of church property, German mediatization 1803 — the state simply taking the church's assets; each wave produced martyrs, emigrants, and the long conservative reaction
- **Suppression and survival**: the Jesuits were suppressed by the Pope himself under state pressure (1773) — and restored (1814). Even a decapitated order can regenerate if its network (schools, missions, alumni) survives — institutional redundancy again.

## Part 5: Wesley's Accidental Schism (the user's example, unpacked)

The perfect case of institution-building outrunning intent:

1. **Field preaching** ("the world is my parish"): Wesley preached outdoors *by definition outside parish jurisdiction* — technically an ecclesiastical offense; the tactic itself was a jurisdictional bypass (the same move as house meetings: meet where the institution's licensing doesn't reach)
2. **The class-meeting system**: lay-led cell groups for discipline + accountability + collection — he built a *parallel church structure* inside the Church of England while insisting he wasn't leaving it (the dual-org pattern, accidentally)
3. **Lay preachers**: bypassed the ordination monopoly — the bhakti/Lollard move in Anglican form
4. **The 1784 ordinations**: needing clergy for America (Bishop of London refused — post-independence there were no Anglican bishops there), Wesley ordained ministers himself — **a church-law violation he justified as emergency**. He intended Methodists to remain Anglican; his own infrastructure made separation inevitable (he died 1791; formal separation followed).
5. **The hymns**: Charles Wesley's ~6,000 hymns — vernacular theology delivered by *singing* (doctrine as earworm — information infrastructure; a people who sing the same theology are doctrinally bound whether or not the institution admits it)

**The mechanic**: Wesley's problem was that his counter-infrastructure worked *too well* — by the time separation happened, Methodism already had its own clergy, cells, doctrine-via-song, and revenue. Schism was post hoc recognition of a parallel institution that already existed. This is how most schisms actually happen: the parallel structure gets built for tactical reasons, then the split is just acknowledgment.

## Part 6: Pacifist Nonconformity — the Refusal of Deference-Tech

- **Quakers**: refused oaths (Matthew 5:34 — no swearing, period), refused hat honor (wouldn't doff to social superiors), refused tithes — three refusals that were *each* small but total: they declined the whole deference-signaling system. Imprisoned by the thousands; founded Pennsylvania as exit-community.
- **Mennonites/Anabaptists**: Schleitheim Confession 1527 — nonresistance as doctrine *in response to* the sword-bearing churches' violence; the *Martyrs Mirror* (1660) — a martyrology as movement-memory infrastructure: the community's suffering documented and bound as its identity book (cf. Foxe's Book of Martyrs on the Protestant side — martyr-narrative as loyalty tech)
- **The pattern**: pacifist sects are *non-participation* movements — they win not by defeating power but by declining its premise (the state's core tech: oath-binding, military service, tithe-extraction). Refusal is the ultimate structure test — it costs everything and proves everything.

## Game Mechanic Proposals

### Mechanic 1: Doctrine as scored conflict (creed-builder)
Belief-system components: each doctrine slot records which position it excludes. Factions accumulate `doctrine_set` by winning argument-events (councils, disputations) — the creed as a ledger of victories. Splinter factions form around the losing clauses (each clause carries a `heresy_vector` — the defeated position that can resurface).

### Mechanic 2: Council events
Periodic dispute-resolution events: factions present positions → vote/influence resolves the canon → losers become heretics (exit, persecution, or sect-formation). The negotiation IS the doctrine production — modeled as a campaign type (church councils as org battles).

### Mechanic 3: Pain/suffering management stat
`affliction_load` on citizens; the faith institution supplies `suffering_meaning` (theodicy — converts grievance into endurance/devotion). Technology removes affliction → the doctrine depreciates → crisis-of-meaning events (the anesthesia pattern: healing tech undermines the theodicy that explained the suffering it couldn't fix).

### Mechanic 4: Sacraments as membership infrastructure
`baptism` = citizenship assignment (who controls it controls the rolls); `communion` = membership enforcement (exclusion = exile); both are **levers** — a faction controlling the sacraments controls the community's self-reproduction. Alternative-rite events (believers' baptism, lay communion) are sovereignty claims — that's why they triggered capital response.

### Mechanic 5: Interdict-type collective punishment
Power-holders can shut down *all* services to a region (interdict, embargo, platform-wide ban) — legitimacy-costly but devastating; counters: substitute service-providers (underground priests — the Waldensian barbes), or surrender (John).

### Mechanic 6: Accidental schism / parallel-church emergence
Org building parallel infrastructure (cells, lay leaders, vernacular texts) accrues `separation_pressure`; above threshold, schism event fires *whether or not the leadership wants it* — the structure dictates the politics. Player option: suppress the parallel infra (lose capacity) or ride it (lose unity).

### Mechanic 7: Deference-refusal as action class
Pacifist/nonconformist actions: refuse oath, refuse honor-ritual, refuse tribute — individually tiny, collectively total (the Quaker pattern). High-cost, high-signal actions that mark the movement as outside the premise — pure commitment-display tech.
